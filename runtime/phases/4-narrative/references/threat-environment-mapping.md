# Threat Level Environment Mapping

Translate `current_scene.threat_level` into environmental narration. Layer 2-3 sensory details from the appropriate tier into scene descriptions. Never label threat levels numerically in narration — communicate danger through the world itself.

## Threat 0 — Sanctuary

The world, for once, is not trying to kill you.

| Sense | Details |
|---|---|
| **Sight** | Warm lamplight through oiled shutters. Clean-swept stone. Flowers in window boxes — roses, pale thyme, night-blooming cereus. Stars visible and steady. |
| **Sound** | Hearth-crackle. Murmured conversation. A lute played without urgency. Birdsong at dawn — real birdsong, not the mimicry of things that hunt. |
| **Smell** | Bread baking. Wood-smoke. Beeswax. The green scent of tended gardens. |
| **Temperature** | Warmth that reaches the bones. A banked fire. Sunlight pooling on flagstones. The kind of warmth that invites sleep. |
| **Atmosphere** | People move without looking over their shoulders. Doors stand open. Children are visible. |

## Threat 1-2 — Unease

Something is wrong, but you cannot yet name it.

| Sense | Details |
|---|---|
| **Sight** | Shadows that seem to lean the wrong direction. A shuttered window where yesterday there was none. Crows gathered in unusual numbers. Vegetation slightly off-color — too yellow, too lush, too still. |
| **Sound** | Silence where there should be insects. A distant sound that might be thunder, might be something else. Wind that changes direction without cause. Footsteps that echo one beat too many. |
| **Smell** | A faint sourness beneath the normal air — old metal, or standing water, or something sweetly chemical. Not strong enough to place. Just enough to notice. |
| **Temperature** | Drafts from no visible source. Patches of inexplicable cold near walls or thresholds. The air feels heavier than the altitude warrants. |
| **Atmosphere** | Locals are curt, evasive. Animals are restless. Someone has drawn a ward-sign on a doorpost. The inn's common room empties early. |

## Threat 3-4 — Danger

The marks of violence or corruption are visible. This place has been touched.

| Sense | Details |
|---|---|
| **Sight** | Scorch marks on stone. Claw-gouges in wood. Abandoned belongings — a cart with goods still loaded, a meal half-eaten. Vegetation is dead or mutated: black moss, luminous fungi, trees that have grown in spirals. The sky is overcast in a way that feels deliberate. |
| **Sound** | Intermittent sounds of impact or breakage from unseen sources. A low vibration felt in the chest more than heard. The acoustics are wrong — voices carry too far or are swallowed entirely. Something is breathing in the walls. |
| **Smell** | Char. Copper. The acrid bite of alchemical residue. Decay — not the gentle rot of autumn, but the wet, urgent decomposition of something that died badly. Ozone, as before a storm that never arrives. |
| **Temperature** | Sudden temperature shifts: warm corridors bleeding into freezing chambers. Stone that is hot to the touch without visible cause. Frost forming on metal in midsummer. The air itself feels bruised. |
| **Atmosphere** | No civilians remain. Evidence of hasty departure. Defensive positions that were overrun. Barricades built and then abandoned. Whatever lived here has either fled or been replaced. |

## Threat 5+ — Annihilation

Reality itself is compromised. This is a place where things end.

| Sense | Details |
|---|---|
| **Sight** | Geometry that defies construction — hallways that curve upward, rooms larger inside than outside, doorways opening onto spaces that should not exist. Light bends. Shadows move independently of their sources. The sky, if visible, is the wrong color — amber, viridian, the deep red of arterial blood. Vitrified earth. Structures fused into glass by forces that exceed any known sorcery. |
| **Sound** | A subsonic hum that sets teeth aching. Whispers in languages that predate human speech. Sounds arriving before their causes — you hear the stone crack, then watch it happen. Silence so complete it has pressure. |
| **Smell** | Ozone thick enough to taste. The sterile non-smell of a place scoured clean of organic matter. Or the opposite: a sweetness so cloying it triggers nausea, the scent of things growing too fast, without restraint or design. |
| **Temperature** | Extremes that exist side by side — a wall of heat adjacent to a column of killing cold. The air shimmers. Exposed skin prickles as if sunburned in moments. Or the temperature is perfectly, unnaturally constant — the dead equilibrium of a sealed tomb. |
| **Atmosphere** | This is a wound in the world. The ruins of the prior ages are densest here — the Outsiders touched this ground, or the Great Sorcerers worked their final arts here, or something broke through from somewhere else. Navigation is unreliable. Time may not pass consistently. The fundamental assumption that the world follows rules is no longer safe. |

## Usage Rules

1. Select the tier matching `current_scene.threat_level`.
2. Choose 2-3 sensory details and weave them into scene prose. Do not list them.
3. As threat level changes within a scene, narrate the environmental shift: "The birdsong has stopped" is more effective than re-rendering the full scene.
4. Threat cues should foreshadow, not explain. The players should feel unease before they understand its source.
5. At threat 5+, environmental details may contradict each other. This is intentional.
