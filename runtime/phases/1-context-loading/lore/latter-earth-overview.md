# Latter Earth Overview

## §what-is-the-latter-earth

The Latter Earth is a world immeasurably old. Uncounted ages of civilization have risen, flourished, and fallen into ruin since the first sentient beings shaped fire and stone. The current age is merely the latest thin layer atop geological strata of collapsed empires, forgotten sciences, and dead gods. No scholar can say with certainty how many ages have passed — the deepest ruins contain artifacts that defy all understanding, built by hands that may not have been human.

## §the-legacy

The Legacy is the collective inheritance of all prior ages: ultra-technology, sorcerous workings, engineered species, reshaped landscapes, and stranger things besides. Legacy artifacts range from the mundane (self-sharpening blades, ever-burning lanterns) to the terrifying (world-engines buried beneath mountains, orbital constructs visible as wandering stars). Most Legacy technology is broken, depleted, or dangerously unstable. The few working artifacts are priceless — wars have been fought over a single functioning device.

Legacy ruins dot the landscape. Some are merely ancient buildings; others are vast underground complexes, pocket dimensions, or regions where the laws of nature were permanently altered. All are dangerous. The creatures that inhabit them — whether original guardians, evolved vermin, or things drawn by residual energies — are uniformly lethal.

## §the-current-age

The current age is one of slow, painful rebuilding. The last great catastrophe — referred to by most scholars as the Shattering, though its true nature is [UNKNOWN] — occurred roughly six centuries ago and collapsed what remained of the prior age's civilization. Humanity and its kindred species survived in scattered enclaves, gradually reforming into kingdoms, city-states, and tribal confederations.

Technology has stabilized at roughly a medieval level. Iron, steel, wood, and stone form the basis of daily life. Agriculture supports growing populations, trade routes connect distant lands, and nascent scholarship attempts to recover fragments of lost knowledge. But the Legacy intrudes everywhere: a farmer's plow strikes an ancient conduit; a road follows the path of a vanished rail-line; a castle is built from stones cut by inhuman hands in a forgotten epoch.

## §magic

Magic is real, potent, and feared. Arcane traditions draw upon the residual energies seeded into the world by prior ages — energies that permeate certain locations, materials, and bloodlines. Practitioners spend years mastering the disciplined mental frameworks needed to channel these forces without being consumed by them.

The major traditions include:

- **High Magic**: scholarly, systematic, requiring years of study. Practiced in academies and towers.
- **Elementalism**: communion with the primal forces bound into the world's fabric during [UNKNOWN] age.
- **Necromancy**: manipulation of the boundary between life and death, universally reviled but persistently practiced.
- **Blood Sorcery**: drawing power from living sacrifice, the most dangerous and corrupting path.
- **Hedge Magic**: folk traditions, herbal lore, and minor workings passed down through oral tradition.

Spellcasting is exhausting and limited. Even powerful mages can manage only a few significant workings per day before risking burnout, madness, or worse. Magic cannot be mass-produced or industrialized — each casting is a personal act of will.

## §danger

The Latter Earth is genuinely dangerous. Wilderness between settlements is not pastoral countryside but lethal terrain. Monsters — many of them Legacy creations or their descendants — inhabit forests, mountains, and wastes. Banditry is endemic where law fails. Disease, famine, and natural disaster are constant threats.

Ruins are worse. The deeper and older the ruin, the more dangerous its inhabitants and traps. Automated defense systems still function after millennia. Engineered predators breed in the dark. Sorcerous contamination warps flesh and mind. The treasures within are real, but so is the cost of claiming them.
