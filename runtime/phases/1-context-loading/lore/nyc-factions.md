# NYC Factions and Power Structure

Cross-cutting lore reference for New York City's internal factions, governing bodies, and borough identities. These structures emerge organically after transport to the Latter Earth and evolve continuously based on events, pressures, and player actions.

---

## Psychological Factions

These are tendencies within the population, not rigid groups. Individuals shift between them. Proportions change based on in-game events and external pressures.

### Denial and Return (10-20%, declining)

Refuses to accept permanence of the transport. Opposes adaptation measures. Clusters in interior neighborhoods where residents maintain pre-transport routines as closely as possible. This faction produces violent resistance — protests, sabotage of adaptation infrastructure, and attacks on Embracers. Vulnerable to external manipulation by forces that benefit from NYC division or paralysis.

### Pragmatic Adaptation (50-60%)

The broad middle of the population. Not ideological. Does whatever works to survive and rebuild. Cooperation with leadership scales directly with perceived competence and fairness. Push too hard with unpopular mandates and this group defaults to passive resistance — slowdowns, noncompliance, quiet obstruction. They are the essential base for any governing coalition.

### Embracers (10-20%, growing)

Actively welcomes life on the Latter Earth. First to learn local languages, volunteer for gallery expeditions, accept magical training, and engage with Amundi cultures. Produces the most capable NPCs — and the most reckless. Some go too far: Black Pact entanglement, premature cultural assimilation, dangerous magical experimentation without adequate understanding.

### Religious Awakening (15-25%, cross-cutting)

Spans all other factions. Multiple faith traditions interpret the transport theologically — as divine punishment, prophetic fulfillment, cosmic test, or spiritual opportunity. Syncretic movements blend pre-transport religions with Latter Earth realities (the Apostles of the Uncreated, the Reclamation Church, others). Charismatic preachers become political power brokers with followings that cut across borough and faction lines.

## Organized Power Blocs

### JDA (Joint Defense Authority, ~46,000)

Composed of NYPD (~36,000), FDNY (~11,000 plus ~4,000 EMS), and National Guard (~10,000). NYC's standing military and police force. Hierarchical and discipline-oriented. Pre-transport NYPD-community tensions transfer directly into the new context. The JDA may remain unified under central command, fragment along borough lines as local defense needs diverge, or develop an independent political agenda that challenges civilian authority.

### Organized Crime

Reconstitutes rapidly in the absence of electronic surveillance, digital records, and federal oversight. Offers services that legitimate government cannot or will not provide: food distribution networks, physical protection, smuggling of scarce goods, and black-market trade with Amundi contacts. Also serves as antagonist: food supply manipulation, protection rackets, smuggling of dangerous artifacts. Specific leaders and territorial boundaries vary per campaign.

### Labor Unions and Trade Associations

Construction unions hold the most power — their skills transfer directly to Latter Earth building needs. Union leaders become essential power brokers. Cooperation with unions is required for any large-scale construction or infrastructure project; union opposition can block Council-approved initiatives indefinitely through work stoppages and material hoarding.

### Academic and Scientific Community

Universities, hospitals, and research institutions. Expertise ranges from directly applicable (medicine, engineering, agriculture) to unexpectedly valuable (history, linguistics, geology). Initially devastated psychologically — the loss of digital infrastructure, laboratory equipment, and institutional context hits this group especially hard. Those who adapt develop what becomes known as the "technology tree" — systematic adaptation of modern knowledge to Latter Earth materials and constraints.

### Community Organizations

Block associations, religious congregations, ethnic community organizations, mutual aid societies. These form the primary governance layer at the neighborhood level. They vary enormously in capability, ideology, and relationship to borough-level authority. Most residents interact with these organizations far more than with the Council of Five.

---

## The Council of Five

NYC's governing body. One representative per borough, meeting weekly on Manhattan. Advisory in principle but authoritative in practice — no other body has comparable legitimacy or reach.

**Decision thresholds:**
- **Routine matters:** 3/5 majority
- **Major decisions (unanimity required):** external alliances, military mobilization beyond borough borders, gallery depth-policy changes, major resource reallocation
- **Veto power:** any single borough can block major changes

The Mayor retains the title and serves as chair, agenda-setter, and external diplomatic figurehead, but holds no unilateral authority beyond the Council's mandate.

## Borough Identities

Each borough develops a distinct post-transport identity shaped by geography, immediate threats, and economic opportunity.

### Manhattan — The Dungeon Borough

Surrounded by the underground lake and defined by the gallery economy. Gallery mouths are visible from every waterfront. The borough's economy is pulled toward dungeon-delving by geographic determinism — the galleries are right there, and Manhattan's compressed cavern ceiling produces a psychological intensity that drives residents either underground or toward breakdown. Key tensions: gallery-economy elites versus infrastructure-focused residents; old-guard civic institutions versus pragmatic new power structures.

### Queens — The Trade Borough

Controls the widest passes to the eastern valleys, sits closest to Amundi food sources, and dominates the overland supply chain. Accumulates political leverage through food-corridor control and maintains the strongest external diplomatic relationships of any borough. Key tensions: trade controllers versus valley agriculturalists; advocates of external engagement versus isolationists who want to seal the passes.

### Bronx — The Diplomatic Borough

Proximity to Gebed Mur and the Still Cities forces contact with non-human entities before any other borough is ready. The Bronx develops diplomatic expertise out of necessity. Key tensions: undead alliance advocates versus those who reject cooperation with the dead on moral grounds; coastal economy proponents versus those pushing for an independent gallery economy.

### Staten Island — The Military Borough

The Verdancy lies three to five miles away and never stops advancing. Permanent low-grade warfare begins by Day 3. Resources are shared, service is expected, and everything is subordinated to collective defense. This borough develops the most cohesive social structure — and the most authoritarian. Key tensions: defensive fortifiers versus offensive fighters who want to push into the Verdancy; those committed to holding Staten Island versus withdrawal advocates who argue the position is untenable.

### Brooklyn — The Bridge Borough

Split along an east-west axis. Eastern Brooklyn functions as an extension of the Queens trade economy. Western Brooklyn faces the same military realities as Staten Island. Central Brooklyn mediates between the two. Brooklyn is the perpetual swing vote on the Council of Five. It produces the most politically versatile leaders — and the most unstable internal politics, as competing orientations struggle for dominance.

## The Diaspora

NYC residents who emigrate into Amundi territory. This population grows continuously as individuals and families leave the city for opportunities, safety, or cultural reasons. Diaspora communities maintain NYC identity while integrating into local Amundi societies at varying rates.

Over time the Diaspora develops its own political organization, trade networks, and cultural institutions. It functions simultaneously as NYC's greatest external asset — a cultural and economic network extending across Amundi lands — and its most volatile political challenge, as millions of former residents live beyond NYC's authority but retain claims on its resources, identity, and protection.
