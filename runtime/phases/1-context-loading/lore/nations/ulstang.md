# Ulstang

## §history
Ulstang is a harsh sea-raider culture occupying the frozen archipelago north of the mainland. For centuries, the Ulstangi were feared as merciless raiders who descended on coastal settlements in their longships, taking what they needed and burning the rest. Two generations ago, a visionary jarl named Sigra Bloodless negotiated trade agreements with the nations her people had terrorized, arguing that commerce was more profitable than plunder. Raiding has declined but not vanished — some clans consider trade a betrayal of ancestral ways.

## §geography
A chain of rocky, windswept islands in the Nightwater. The terrain is volcanic — black basalt cliffs, geysers, and sparse tundra. Summers are cool and brief; winters are dark, with the sun barely rising for months. The surrounding waters are rich in fish and whale. Glaciers cover the interior of the larger islands. Hot springs provide the only reliable warmth.

## §government
A thing-based system. Each island is governed by a jarl elected by free warriors at the local thing (assembly). A grand thing meets annually to settle inter-island disputes and elect a high jarl for matters of collective defense. Women who bear arms have full political rights. Thralls (bondservants) have limited protections but no vote.

## §culture
Ulstangi culture glorifies courage, endurance, and personal reputation. Saga-telling preserves history and law in oral form. Hospitality is sacred within the longhouse but does not extend to the open sea. Blood feuds are common and regulated by complex rules of compensation. Craftsmanship in shipbuilding and bone carving is highly respected. Food is preserved fish, whale blubber, and hard bread.

## §cultural-implications
Combat prowess and seamanship earn respect. Ulstangi NPCs value directness and despise cowardice. Showing fear invites contempt. Reputation is everything — a PC's deeds will be remembered and recounted. Gift-giving establishes social bonds; refusing a gift is an insult.

## §sensory-palette
Ulstang is wind and water — salt spray on black rock, the shriek of seabirds, the groan of ice. The air bites with cold that never fully relents. Longhouses glow amber in the perpetual twilight of winter. The smell of smoked fish, tar, and wet wool permeates every settlement. In summer, the midnight sun paints the sea in molten gold.

## §voice-notes
Ulstangi speak in clipped, rhythmic sentences with a rising inflection. They use kennings — poetic compound words: the sea is the "whale-road," a sword is a "wound-snake." Common expressions: "The sea takes what it will" and "A name outlives a body." They boast openly and expect others to do the same.

## §adventure-hooks
- A longship has returned from a raid with a Legacy artifact that is slowly transforming its crew. They are becoming stronger, faster — and less human.
- Traditionalist clans are planning a massive raid against the mainland to prove that the old ways are superior to trade. The high jarl needs the plan stopped without sparking civil war.
- A glacier has retreated, revealing a perfectly preserved ship of unknown design, far larger than any Ulstangi vessel. It is not made of wood.
- Something is killing whales in the northern waters — not hunting them, but destroying them. Whale carcasses wash ashore bearing wounds no weapon could make.

## §unknown
[UNKNOWN] — The oldest sagas speak of a time before the islands, when the Ulstangi sailed a different sea under different stars. Whether this is metaphor, myth, or a literal account of a prior age is debated by skalds. Certain ritual songs, sung only at midwinter, contain words in no known language that produce a physical sensation of movement when chanted in unison.
