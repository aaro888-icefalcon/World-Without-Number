# Qasir

## §history

Qasir grew wealthy as the Amundi world's premier maritime trading kingdom, its position on the Gulf of Amund making it the natural gateway between the Amundi kingdoms and the wider world including the Atlantean Main. The Emir's dynasty has ruled from Khatin for generations, accumulating wealth through trade tariffs, merchant fleet operations, and strategic neutrality in Amundi conflicts. This prosperity attracted not only legitimate commerce but also the patient attention of Sarx, whose agents have spent years embedding themselves within Qasir's merchant houses — a compromise that Qasir's authorities have not yet confirmed or fully grasped.

## §geography

Qasir occupies the Gulf of Amund coastline, centered on the port city of Khatin. The gulf provides sheltered harbor for merchant fleets and naval vessels alike. Inland territories support approximately 1.5 million people through a combination of irrigated agriculture and trade-sustained urban populations. The coastline is dotted with smaller trading ports, fishing villages, and the estates of wealthy merchant families. Maritime routes connect Qasir to the Atlantean Main and distant trading partners beyond Amundi waters.

## §government

The Emir rules from the wealthiest court in the Amundi world, supported by a merchant aristocracy whose influence derives from commercial success rather than military power. Governance is transactional — the Emir maintains order, the merchants generate wealth, and both profit. The navy protects trade routes; the army is modest by Amundi standards, relying on mercenaries when ground force is needed. Sarxian agents embedded in merchant houses represent an unacknowledged corruption of this system, their influence growing incrementally through commercial leverage.

## §culture

Qasiran culture celebrates commerce, hospitality, and the art of negotiation. Elaborate courtesy governs all interactions, from street-market haggling to court audiences with the Emir. Wealth is displayed openly but tastefully — silk, spice, fine metalwork. The merchant houses function as extended family networks, and commercial reputation is the foundation of social standing. Qasir's cosmopolitan ports welcome traders from across the known world, making it the most culturally diverse of the Amundi kingdoms.

- Spice-laden air drifting through covered bazaars — cinnamon, cardamom, saffron, and unfamiliar aromatics from distant ports
- Silk-draped courts where the Emir receives petitioners amid cushions, brass lamps, and the murmur of fountains
- Merchant vessels at anchor, their holds being loaded and unloaded by dockworkers chanting in rhythm
- The call of gulls over Khatin's waterfront, mingling with the clink of coin and the scratch of ledger-quills

## §voice-notes

Elaborate courtesy frames every exchange. Merchant precision underlies the flowery language — Qasirans never lose track of the numbers beneath the pleasantries. Underlying calculation governs what is said and what is omitted. Hospitality formulas are lengthy and sincere; rushing them is an insult. The Emir speaks with paternal warmth that conceals sharp commercial instincts. Sarxian-compromised merchants are indistinguishable in manner from their uncompromised peers — this is by design.
