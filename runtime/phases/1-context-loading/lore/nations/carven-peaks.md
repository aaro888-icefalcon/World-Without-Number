# Carven Peaks

## §history
Nine and a half million people woke one morning in the city of New York — a metropolis from a world called Earth — and found themselves somewhere else entirely. The translocation was instantaneous and total: every building, bridge, road, vehicle, boat, and human being within the five boroughs was ripped from one reality and deposited into the Latter Earth. No warning. No explanation. No return.

The first hours were defined by catastrophe. Every electronic device, every motor, every generator ceased to function — permanently. The physics of the Latter Earth do not permit such technologies; a phenomenon residents would come to call Legacy decay rendered them inert. Combustion engines, power grids, water treatment plants, elevators, refrigeration, communications — all dead simultaneously. Firearms, hand tools, printed books, unpowered boats, and simple mechanical devices continued to work. Everything else became dead weight.

On Day 1, an Imperator surge — a violent eruption of hostile entities from the gallery system beneath Manhattan — killed between 400 and 1,000 people. The surge established the galleries as an active, persistent threat and forced immediate militarization of the borough's interior. Over the next seven days, five revelations cascaded through the population in waves of escalating panic: the world outside the cavern was not Earth; magic was real and some residents could now wield it; the city's food supply had a finite clock measured in weeks; the old government structures could not help; and the dead, if left unburned, could rise.

By Day 14 through Day 30, the borough federation had crystallized into a functional if fragile governing body. The gallery economy produced its first resource cycle — ore, Legacy artifacts, and salvageable materials hauled from the depths. The western front against the Verdancy hardened into a permanent defensive line. New York City became the Carven Peaks, and its people began the work of survival.

## §geography
Manhattan occupies a colossal natural cavern within the Carven Peaks mountain range, approximately 14 miles long and 2.5 miles wide. The cavern ceiling hovers roughly 200 feet above street level — close enough to feel oppressive, far enough to disappear into gloom. Bioluminescent mineral deposits embedded in the ceiling rock provide a perpetual dim blue-green twilight that never brightens and never fades. There is no sunrise. There is no sunset. There is only the blue.

An underground freshwater lake surrounds the island of Manhattan, stretching 500 to 800 feet from the shoreline to the cavern walls. The water is cold — approximately 45 degrees Fahrenheit — clear, and drinkable. Carved into the cavern walls above the waterline are 40 to 60 gallery mouths: tunnel entrances of varying size, visibly hewn by intelligent hands in an age before memory. These galleries extend deep into the mountain, their full extent unmapped. Less than 5% of the system has been explored.

The outer boroughs connect to the surface world through mountain passes of varying width and strategic significance. Queens and eastern Brooklyn extend through wide passes into river valleys flowing southeast, providing arable farmland and access to the Amundi trade network. The Bronx reaches through northern passes into cold conifer valleys and proximity to the Gebed Mur — a vast freshwater sea controlled by the Still Cities and their undead navy. Staten Island and western Brooklyn face narrow passes opening toward the Verdancy, a lethal polychrome forest only 3 to 5 miles from the borough perimeter.

Brooklyn is geographically and politically bifurcated: its eastern half participates in the trade economy of the river valleys, while its western half confronts the military reality of the Verdancy front. A subterranean river tunnel approximately 2 miles long connects the underground lake to the eastern valley river system, but a collapsed section currently blocks barge traffic.

## §government
The Council of Five governs the Carven Peaks, with one representative from each borough. The council meets weekly on Manhattan. The pre-translocation Mayor retains the title and serves as chair and figurehead, but real authority flows from the council's majority vote. Routine matters require a simple majority of three out of five votes. Matters of alliance, full military mobilization, or gallery depth-policy require unanimity.

Borough-level autonomy is the practical reality. Each borough is governed by whoever effectively controls it — in some cases elected officials who retained legitimacy, in others strongmen, union bosses, community leaders, or military commanders who seized authority during the crisis. The council provides coordination; the boroughs provide governance.

The Joint Defense Authority (JDA) was reorganized on Day 1 from approximately 46,000 personnel drawn from the NYPD, FDNY, and National Guard units that were present in the city at the time of translocation. The JDA handles external defense, gallery incursion response, and inter-borough security. Borough-level policing remains with local forces.

## §culture
The population has fractured into overlapping psychological factions. Between 50 and 60 percent are Pragmatic Adapters — people who have accepted the new reality and are focused on survival and rebuilding. Ten to 20 percent remain in Denial or cling to belief in a Return to Earth. Another 10 to 20 percent are Embracers who have eagerly adopted the new world's possibilities, including magic. Fifteen to 25 percent have experienced a Religious Awakening, interpreting the translocation through spiritual or prophetic frameworks.

Gallery delvers — those who descend into the tunnel systems beneath Manhattan to extract resources, Legacy artifacts, and raw materials — have become figures of extraordinary social importance. They are the city's primary economic producers, and their status reflects it. The old social hierarchy has inverted: tradespeople, construction workers, plumbers, and electricians are valued far above lawyers, financiers, and office workers. Immigrant communities with strong collective-action traditions — those accustomed to mutual aid, communal labor, and operating outside institutional support — have adapted fastest and hold disproportionate influence.

Cremation of the dead within 24 hours is mandatory. The ambient Legacy field of the Latter Earth reanimates approximately 1 to 5 percent of unburied corpses as feral revenants — mindless, violent, and relentless. Funeral culture has transformed overnight. Grief is real but burial is death.

## §manhattan
Manhattan is called the Dungeon Borough. Its entire economy orbits the galleries — the carved tunnel entrances visible in the cavern walls across the underground lake. Delver crews launch from Manhattan's shores, cross the cold water by rowboat or improvised ferry, and enter the galleries to extract whatever they can find. The compressed cavern ceiling gives Manhattan a claustrophobic intensity that the surface boroughs lack. Political tension runs between the gallery-elite — successful delver captains and the merchants who trade in their haul — and the infrastructure workers who keep the island's water, sanitation, and food distribution functional without electricity.

## §queens
Queens is the Trade Borough. Its wide eastern passes provide the most accessible route to the river valleys and the Amundi trade network beyond. Food flows through Queens; so does leverage. The borough controls the city's primary agricultural corridor and its most reliable external trade relationships. Internal politics revolve around the tension between trade dependency — Queens needs the other boroughs' labor and manufactured goods — and the independence that food control provides. Queens' representative on the Council of Five often holds the swing vote.

## §bronx
The Bronx is the Diplomatic Borough. Northern passes lead to the cold conifer valleys and, beyond them, the Gebed Mur — the freshwater sea where the Still Cities maintain an undead navy. The Bronx is the Carven Peaks' point of contact with the most alien and unsettling of its neighbors. Diplomatic missions to the Still Cities depart from the Bronx. The borough's politics are dominated by the question of alliance: the Still Cities offer military protection against external threats, but their price — and their nature — makes many residents deeply uncomfortable. The undead do not eat, do not sleep, and do not forget. Whether they can be trusted is the defining debate of Bronx political life.

## §staten-island
Staten Island is the Military Borough. From Day 3, when the first Verdancy incursion breached the narrow western passes, Staten Island has been on a permanent war footing. The polychrome forest is lethal, beautiful, and relentless — its flora and fauna press against the borough's perimeter in a low-grade siege that never fully abates. Every resident of Staten Island contributes to collective defense in some capacity. The borough has developed the tightest community cohesion and the most militarized culture of the five boroughs. Its council representative consistently votes for increased JDA funding and aggressive gallery exploration to find weapons capable of pushing the Verdancy back.

## §brooklyn
Brooklyn is the Bridge Borough, split down the middle by geography and allegiance. Eastern Brooklyn participates in the trade economy that flows through Queens — farmland access, river valley commerce, Amundi contact. Western Brooklyn faces the same military reality as Staten Island, with the Verdancy threatening its narrow passes. This east-west split makes Brooklyn the perpetual mediator and political swing vote on the Council of Five. Brooklyn's representative must balance two constituencies with fundamentally different priorities: one wants trade and growth, the other wants walls and weapons. The borough's internal politics are the most fractious of the five, but its bridging role gives it outsized influence in council negotiations.

## §cultural-implications
PCs interacting with Carven Peaks residents encounter a population in collective trauma that has not yet settled into stable culture. Social currency comes from practical capability — what can you do with your hands, what have you brought back from the galleries, what skills do you have that keep people alive. Pre-translocation status (wealth, education, professional title) is nearly worthless and may provoke resentment. Gallery delvers are treated like returning heroes or doomed fools depending on the borough. Mandatory cremation is a non-negotiable social norm; interfering with it or suggesting alternatives will provoke immediate hostility. Borough identity is strong and growing stronger — residents identify as Manhattanite, Queens-born, Bronx-side before they identify as New Yorkers.

## §sensory-palette
Manhattan: the bioluminescent blue-green twilight of the cavern ceiling casts everything in perpetual undersea hues. Sound echoes off carved stone walls and water. The constant low murmur of 1.6 million people living under rock replaces the old noise of traffic. The smell is lake water, damp stone, torch smoke, and the metallic tang of gallery dust. Across the dark water, gallery mouths are visible as black rectangles cut into the cavern walls — dozens of them, watching like empty eye sockets.

Outer boroughs: the sky is wrong. Unfamiliar constellations wheel overhead. Sunlight has a shifted color temperature — slightly amber, slightly too warm. Mountain slopes replace highways at the borough edges. The air smells of pine, cold stone, and alien pollen. Wind carries sounds that have no Earth analogue — the low hum of the Verdancy's bioluminescence, the distant percussion of Still Cities drums across the Gebed Mur.

## §voice-notes
Carven Peaks residents speak modern American English but are rapidly developing new vocabulary. "Gallery" means the underground tunnel system. "Topside" refers to the surface boroughs outside the cavern. "The blue" is the bioluminescent cavern ceiling glow. "Delvers" are gallery explorers. "Legacy" refers to the magical/technological remnants of prior ages, including the force that killed their electronics.

NPC speech patterns retain the direct, fast-paced NYC cadence. Profanity increases under stress, which is constant. Borough-specific slang persists and is intensifying as cultural identity hardens. Manhattanites talk about "going down" (entering galleries) and "the haul." Bronx residents reference "the cold ones" (Still Cities undead) with a mixture of fear and pragmatism. Staten Islanders speak of "the line" (Verdancy perimeter) with grim familiarity. Conversations frequently reference the old world — "back when," "before the move," "on Earth" — with varying degrees of grief, anger, or acceptance.

## §adventure-hooks
- The collapsed section of the subterranean river tunnel could be cleared to enable barge traffic between the underground lake and the eastern valley river system. Multiple factions want it opened — or want it to stay closed. The rubble conceals something unexpected.
- A delver crew has returned from a deep gallery expedition with a Legacy artifact that appears to be a map — but of what? The artifact displays locations that do not match any known gallery floor-plan, and one symbol matches the geographic outline of pre-translocation Manhattan. The gallery-elite want it. The Council wants it. Someone is willing to kill for it.
- The Still Cities have sent an emissary through the northern passes requesting a formal alliance against a mutual threat emerging from the Gebed Mur's depths. The emissary is a revenant — sapient, articulate, and unsettlingly polite. The Bronx council representative supports the alliance. Staten Island's representative does not. The vote is 2-2, and Brooklyn has not decided.
- Verdancy organisms have begun growing inside the western passes in patterns that suggest intelligence rather than mere biological expansion. Staten Island's defense line is holding, but the patterns are changing — and three soldiers on the line have reported hearing music from the forest that no one else can hear.

## §unknown
[UNKNOWN] — Why New York City was transported to the Latter Earth. No resident, no Latter Earth scholar, and no Legacy artifact has provided any explanation for the mechanism or cause of the translocation. Theories range from divine intervention to Legacy system malfunction to deliberate summoning by an unknown agent. None have evidence.

[UNKNOWN] — The full extent of the gallery system beneath Manhattan. Current floor-maps represent less than 5 percent of the explored tunnel network, and the tunnels show signs of extending far deeper and wider than the cavern itself. Who carved them, when, and why remains unknown. Some gallery walls bear inscriptions in no known language.

[UNKNOWN] — Whether the Legacy surge that granted magical abilities to a small percentage of the population on Day 1 will repeat, intensify, or prove to be a one-time event. The surge killed hundreds and empowered dozens. Those who gained abilities do not fully understand them. No pattern has been identified in who was affected and who was not.
