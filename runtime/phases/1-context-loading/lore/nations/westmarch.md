# Westmarch

## §history
Westmarch is the quintessential frontier kingdom — a young, expanding nation on the western edge of the mapped world. Founded by pioneers, soldiers, and second sons with no inheritance, it has grown from a handful of stockade villages into a proper kingdom in just eighty years. The current king, Harald the Builder, is only the third monarch. Westmarch is raw, optimistic, and perpetually short of everything except land and ambition. Beyond its western border lies unmapped wilderness that grows stranger the further one travels.

## §geography
Rolling hills and dense deciduous forest, gradually giving way to untamed wilderness in the west. Rivers flow eastward toward settled lands. The climate is temperate with harsh winters. The eastern regions are cultivated; the western reaches remain wild. Legacy ruins are common but largely unexplored — the settlers have been too busy surviving to investigate them. The frontier moves westward each year.

## §government
A constitutional monarchy. The king rules with the advice of a council of barons — landholders who earned their titles through settlement and defense of frontier territory. New baronies are granted to anyone who can establish and hold a settlement for five years. The law is simple and practical. Justice is administered by traveling magistrates.

## §culture
Westmarchers are pragmatic, hard-working, and fiercely independent. They value competence over breeding and action over words. Community barn-raisings, harvest festivals, and militia musters are the primary social events. Art is folk music, woodcarving, and quilting. Food is hearty and plain — stews, bread, smoked meat. Everyone can handle an axe, and most can handle a sword.

## §cultural-implications
Practical skill checks — building, farming, fighting, woodcraft — earn respect. Westmarcher NPCs are friendly but wary of outsiders who seem useless. Offering to help with work earns trust quickly. Aristocratic airs and academic pretension fall flat. The frontier is dangerous — PCs who underestimate the wilderness pay for it.

## §sensory-palette
Westmarch smells of fresh-cut timber, wood smoke, and turned earth. The sound of axes biting into trees echoes through the forests. Settlements are raw — new-built log cabins, muddy streets, half-cleared fields. Beyond the last farmstead, the forest closes in, dark and ancient. At night, things move in the trees that the settlers do not discuss over dinner.

## §voice-notes
Westmarchers speak plainly with a slight drawl. They use practical metaphors: "That dog won't hunt" and "You can't plow a field by staring at it." Common greeting: "What brings you this far out?" They are laconic by nature — a nod conveys what other cultures need a paragraph to express.

## §adventure-hooks
- A newly established frontier settlement has gone silent. The last message mentioned strange lights in the forest and the sound of singing in a language no one recognized.
- A baron's survey team has mapped a region that should not exist — a valley that does not appear on any map, containing structures of obvious Legacy origin. The baron wants it explored before claiming it.
- Something is driving wildlife eastward in massive, panicked migrations. Whatever is pushing them is moving slowly but inexorably toward settled lands.
- A wanderer has returned from deep in the western wilderness carrying a Legacy device that produces a detailed map of the surrounding terrain. The map shows things that are not visible to the naked eye — including something large, underground, and moving.

## §unknown
[UNKNOWN] — The western wilderness grows genuinely strange beyond the frontier. Scouts report regions where the forest gives way to biomes that should not exist — jungles in temperate latitudes, crystalline formations, areas of reversed gravity. Whether the mapped world is an island of normalcy in a larger, warped landscape, or whether something specific is distorting the western reaches, is an open question that the crown is not eager to publicize.
