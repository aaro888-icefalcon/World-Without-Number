# Farallon

## §history
Farallon began as a pirate haven three centuries ago, when a consortium of freebooters seized a chain of volcanic islands in the Sunward Sea. Over generations, piracy evolved into legitimate trade — or at least plausibly deniable trade. Today Farallon is the Latter Earth's preeminent maritime merchant republic, its fleets connecting distant nations and its banks financing ventures across the known world. The old pirate families still rule, though they now call themselves merchant princes.

## §geography
An archipelago of volcanic islands in the warm Sunward Sea. The main islands are lush and mountainous, with natural deep-water harbors. The climate is tropical, with a rainy season and a dry season. Coral reefs surround the outer islands, making unauthorized approach treacherous. Several smaller islands are uninhabited, harboring Legacy ruins amid dense jungle.

## §government
A merchant republic governed by the Council of Princes — the heads of the twelve wealthiest trading families. The council elects a Doge from among its members to serve a ten-year term. In practice, the Doge is whoever can assemble the most votes through bribery, alliance, and leverage. The navy is the true backbone of Faralloni power.

## §culture
Faralloni culture is mercantile, cosmopolitan, and morally flexible. Every culture is represented in its ports, and tolerance is both a value and a business strategy. Art is lavish and competitive — merchant princes commission grand palaces and public works to demonstrate wealth. Food is exceptional, drawing from every cuisine the trade fleets encounter. Contracts are sacred; personal loyalty is not.

## §cultural-implications
Commerce and negotiation checks thrive here. Faralloni NPCs respect wealth and cleverness. Moral appeals are less effective than financial ones. The black market is vast and semi-tolerated. Speaking multiple languages is an advantage.

## §sensory-palette
Farallon assaults the senses: salt spray and tropical flowers, roasting spices and tar, the creak of rigging and the polyglot roar of dockside markets. The harbor is a forest of masts, the water choked with boats. Colors are vivid — painted hulls, dyed sails, merchants in silks from a dozen nations.

## §voice-notes
Faralloni speak rapidly, blending words from multiple languages mid-sentence. They gesture expansively. Common expressions: "Gold speaks every tongue" and "The tide waits for no debt." They address strangers as "friend" immediately, which means nothing until a contract is signed.

## §adventure-hooks
- A merchant prince has been murdered, and the Council of Princes is deadlocked over succession. Each faction is hiring outside agents to investigate — or to frame a rival.
- A newly discovered island holds a Legacy shipyard with vessels of unknown design. Multiple nations are racing to claim it.
- Pirates — real ones, not the genteel merchant kind — are raiding shipping lanes with impossible speed. Their vessels appear and vanish without trace.
- A Faralloni bank has discovered that a significant percentage of the gold in its vaults has been replaced with perfect counterfeits. The substitution happened gradually over years.

## §unknown
[UNKNOWN] — Beneath the main island's volcanic peak lies a vast flooded cavern containing a Legacy structure of unknown purpose. The merchant princes are aware of it but have been unable to access it. The structure emits a low-frequency hum that can be felt but not heard, and it is slowly growing louder.
