# Eldermark

## §history
Eldermark is the oldest continuously inhabited human settlement in the Fifth Age — a distinction its citizens take very seriously. Founded in the immediate aftermath of the Shattering, when refugees fortified a hilltop and refused to be moved, Eldermark has survived every crisis through sheer stubbornness and a legal tradition so conservative it borders on fossilization. The nation's laws have not been substantively changed in three centuries. This is considered a feature, not a bug.

## §geography
A compact territory of ancient farmland, managed woodlands, and meticulously maintained stone roads centered on the original hilltop fortress-city. The terrain is gentle — rolling hills, small rivers, and orderly villages. The climate is mild and temperate. Everything about the landscape feels old and settled. Legacy ruins in Eldermark territory have been known, catalogued, and sealed for centuries.

## §government
A constitutional monarchy bound by the Ancient Charter — the legal document written by the founders. The monarch's power is strictly limited by precedent. The Assembly of Estates — representing nobles, clergy, merchants, and commons — legislates within the Charter's constraints. Legal scholars (Chartermen) hold enormous influence, as every policy must be justified by Charter interpretation. Change is glacially slow.

## §culture
Eldermarkers are traditionalist, legalistic, and deeply proud of their continuity. "We were here first" is not just a boast but a worldview. Innovation is treated with suspicion. Proper procedure is sacred. The arts favor historical chronicles, heraldic painting, and choral music from centuries-old scores. Food follows ancestral recipes. Fashion changes generationally, not annually. Respect for elders is absolute.

## §cultural-implications
History, law, and etiquette skill checks are valued. Eldermark NPCs respect tradition, proper form, and demonstrated knowledge of precedent. New ideas must be framed as recoveries of older wisdom to gain acceptance. PCs who show respect for the past earn goodwill. Those who advocate rapid change are viewed as dangerous radicals.

## §sensory-palette
Eldermark is sepia-toned — warm stone buildings softened by age, cobblestone streets worn smooth by centuries of footsteps, ancient oaks shading village greens. The air smells of baking bread, old timber, and beeswax polish. Church bells mark the hours. The pace of life is unhurried. Everything looks as though it has always been exactly as it is.

## §voice-notes
Eldermarkers speak in formal, measured tones using slightly archaic vocabulary. They cite precedent habitually: "As was established in the year of..." Common expressions: "The Charter provides" and "We endure." They are polite, firm, and immovable. Arguing with an Eldermarker about tradition is like arguing with a stone wall — and about as productive.

## §adventure-hooks
- A sealed Legacy ruin that has been stable for centuries has begun to emit sounds. The Assembly is debating whether the Charter permits opening it. The debate may take years. The sounds are getting louder.
- An Eldermark historian has discovered evidence that the Ancient Charter was not written by the founders but copied from a pre-Shattering document. If true, the nation's legal foundation is a fiction.
- A neighboring nation is building a road that crosses Eldermark's border by fifty feet. By Charter law, this is an act of war. By common sense, it is a surveying error. The Assembly is divided.
- The royal line is about to fail — the monarch has no heir and is dying. The Charter's succession provisions are ambiguous, and three claimants have emerged, each with a valid legal argument.

## §unknown
[UNKNOWN] — The founders' hilltop was not chosen randomly. Beneath the fortress-city lies a Legacy structure that the founders sealed and built over. The Ancient Charter contains a coded passage that no Charterman has deciphered. Some believe it describes the structure's purpose. Others believe it is a warning.
