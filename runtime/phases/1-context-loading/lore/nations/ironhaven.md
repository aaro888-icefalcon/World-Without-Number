# Ironhaven

## §history
Ironhaven is a fortress-nation built to guard a mountain pass against an enemy that no longer exists — or so most believe. Founded three centuries ago when a coalition of kingdoms built a massive fortress to block an invasion route used by creatures from the deep earth, Ironhaven grew from a military installation into a permanent settlement. The creatures were eventually driven back and the deep passages sealed, but Ironhaven remained, evolving into an independent city-state that derives its identity and income from its position as the most heavily fortified location in the Latter Earth.

## §geography
A massive fortress complex occupying a mountain pass, with civilian settlements built into the surrounding cliff faces and valleys. The pass itself is blocked by concentric walls, each higher than the last. The fortress incorporates Legacy defensive systems — some functional, others dormant. The climate is cold and windy. The surrounding mountains are barren granite. Below the fortress, sealed passages descend into the deep earth.

## §government
A military republic. The Castellan — elected by the officer corps — holds executive authority. A Senate of veterans legislates. Citizenship requires military service. The fortress's defense is the state's primary purpose; all policy is evaluated against this criterion. Engineering and military innovation are heavily funded.

## §culture
Ironhaveners are proud, disciplined, and defined by their walls. The fortress is not just their home but their identity. Architecture is both art and defense — every building is designed to serve as a defensive position. Engineering is the supreme profession. Social status correlates with one's contribution to the fortress's strength. Festivals celebrate historical sieges that were repelled. Food is standardized military fare, reliable if uninspired.

## §cultural-implications
Engineering, fortification, and military skill checks are valued. Ironhaven NPCs assess visitors as potential threats or allies. Demonstrating knowledge of defensive architecture earns instant respect. The sealed passages are a sensitive topic — some believe the threat will return, others consider it superstition. PCs who ask too many questions about the deep passages attract attention.

## §sensory-palette
Ironhaven is stone and steel — massive walls of dressed granite, iron-banded gates, the clang of repair hammers on ancient battlements. The wind howls through the pass constantly. The air smells of stone dust, weapon oil, and the ozone crackle of dormant Legacy defense systems. At night, watch-fires line every wall, and the rhythmic boot-falls of patrols echo through the fortress.

## §voice-notes
Ironhaveners speak in clipped military cadence. They use fortification metaphors: "That argument has no foundation" and "Shore up your position before advancing." Common greeting: "The walls hold." They are laconic, professional, and assess threats constantly — even in casual conversation, their eyes sweep the room.

## §adventure-hooks
- One of the seals on the deep passages has broken. Something is coming up from below — not the creatures of old, but something new. The Castellan needs the breach assessed before committing forces.
- A Legacy defense system has activated autonomously, targeting everything that moves in a section of the fortress. Disabling it requires accessing the system's control node — which is inside the kill zone.
- An engineer has discovered that the fortress's foundations incorporate Legacy structures far older than the fortress itself. The pass was fortified before — multiple times, by different builders, across different ages. The same enemy may have been the reason each time.
- A diplomatic delegation from a foreign power wants to study Ironhaven's defenses, claiming they face a similar threat. The Castellan suspects espionage. The delegation insists the threat is real and imminent.
