# Vorduhn

## §geography
Vorduhn occupies a dense, ancient forest stretching across a highland plateau in the continental interior. The canopy is so thick that the forest floor exists in perpetual twilight. Massive trees, some over a thousand years old, form natural pillars hundreds of feet tall. The climate is cool and damp, with frequent mist that clings to the understory. Clearings are rare and considered sacred.

## §government
A decentralized tribal confederation governed by the Rootmoot, a council of clan elders who gather at the Heartwood — an enormous tree at the forest's center that predates human settlement. Each clan governs its own territory autonomously, and the Rootmoot addresses only matters affecting the entire nation. Decisions require unanimous consent, a process that is slow but produces remarkable stability.

## §culture
Vorduhn society is organized around the forest itself. Settlements are built into and among the great trees, with walkways and bridges connecting platforms at canopy level. Clan identity is paramount, traced through matrilineal descent. Woodcraft, herbalism, and beast-taming are the most respected skills. Vorduhni are quiet, watchful, and deeply suspicious of outsiders who do not understand the forest's ways.

## §economy
Vorduhn exports rare hardwoods, medicinal herbs, and forest products — amber, resins, furs, and wild honey — that cannot be obtained elsewhere. They practice sustainable harvesting enforced by strict clan law; no tree is felled without planting three replacements. Trade is conducted at border markets, as foreigners are rarely permitted deep into the forest. The Vorduhni have little use for coinage, preferring barter.

## §military
Vorduhni are unmatched in forest warfare. Clan rangers patrol the canopy with longbows and poisoned darts, invisible among the branches. Invading armies find themselves lost, harassed, and slowly destroyed without ever engaging a visible enemy. The forest itself serves as a defensive weapon; the Vorduhni know every path, and outsiders know none.

## §religion
The Vorduhni practice animist worship, believing that every tree, stream, and stone in the forest possesses a spirit. The Heartwood tree is venerated as the forest's living soul. Druids serve as intermediaries between the clans and the spirits, performing rituals of appeasement before any harvest or construction. The dead are buried among tree roots so their essence feeds new growth.

## §history
Vorduhni oral histories claim the clans have inhabited the forest since the end of the Third Age, retreating beneath the canopy when the wider world collapsed. Archaeological evidence suggests the forest itself may be artificial — planted or accelerated by Legacy systems during an earlier age. Two centuries ago, an expansionist neighbor attempted to clear-cut the eastern border; the Vorduhni repelled them in a guerrilla campaign that lasted four years.

## §current-affairs
The Heartwood tree has begun producing fruit for the first time in living memory. The druids are divided over whether this is a blessing or a warning. Animals near the Heartwood have grown unusually bold and intelligent.
