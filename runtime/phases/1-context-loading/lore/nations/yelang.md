# Yelang

## §history
Yelang is an ancient empire in the far east, claiming continuity with a Third Age predecessor state — a claim partially supported by archaeological evidence. Isolated by vast mountain ranges and dense jungle, Yelang developed independently from the western nations for millennia. Contact with the west was reestablished only two centuries ago via maritime trade routes. Yelang views itself as the center of civilization and regards western nations with polite condescension. Its internal politics are labyrinthine, driven by competing court factions, scholarly academies, and military governors.

## §geography
A vast territory encompassing river valleys, terraced mountains, subtropical lowlands, and dense bamboo forests. The eastern coast faces an ocean that extends to [UNKNOWN] — Yelangzi explorers have found scattered islands but no far shore. The climate ranges from temperate in the north to tropical in the south. Rice paddies dominate the lowlands. The mountain interior is sparsely populated and rich in Legacy ruins of a distinctive style found nowhere else.

## §government
An imperial bureaucracy. The Jade Emperor rules from the Forbidden Citadel, advised by a vast administrative apparatus organized into ministries. Officials are selected through competitive examinations of prodigious difficulty. The military is powerful but subordinate to civil authority. Provincial governors wield significant local power. Court intrigue between factions — the Scholar-Ministers, the Eunuch Secretariat, and the Military Council — shapes all national policy.

## §culture
Yelangzi culture values harmony, hierarchy, filial piety, and scholarly achievement. Calligraphy is the supreme art. Tea culture is elaborate and codified. Poetry composition is expected of all educated persons. Martial arts traditions are ancient and diverse. Food is a fine art with regional variations of extraordinary complexity. Face — personal and family honor — governs all social interactions.

## §cultural-implications
Etiquette and scholarly skill checks are essential. Yelangzi NPCs are courteous but evaluate foreigners against their own cultural standards. Loss of face is devastating. PCs who demonstrate scholarly accomplishment or martial arts skill earn respect. Crude behavior is tolerated from barbarians (all foreigners) but limits access to higher social circles.

## §sensory-palette
Yelang is mist and jade — green mountains veiled in cloud, the scent of jasmine tea and sandalwood incense, the soft rustle of silk robes. Cities are dense and vertical, with curved rooflines and painted eaves. The sound of zither music drifts from scholars' courtyards. Markets are orderly, colorful, and fragrant with spices and lacquerwork. In the mountains, temples cling to cliff faces above waterfalls.

## §voice-notes
Yelangzi speak with precise, formal diction. They use literary allusions and nature metaphors: "The bamboo bends but does not break" and "Autumn comes for every dynasty." Common greeting: a bow calibrated to the relative status of both parties. They consider loudness barbaric and silence a form of eloquence.

## §adventure-hooks
- A provincial governor has declared independence, claiming to have unearthed a Legacy weapon that makes him invulnerable. The court needs the claim verified before committing troops.
- An ancient temple in the mountains has opened for the first time in centuries. Monks who entered have not returned, but their voices can be heard chanting from within — in a language that predates Yelangzi.
- The Jade Emperor's health is failing, and no heir has been named. The three court factions are maneuvering for control. Foreign agents are attempting to influence the outcome.
- Yelangzi explorers returning from the eastern ocean report finding an island that matches descriptions in Third Age texts of a Sorcerer-King's retreat. They brought back a single artifact. It is alive.

## §unknown
[UNKNOWN] — The Forbidden Citadel's innermost chambers contain a Legacy device that the Jade Emperor consults before every major decision. The device has been operational since the Third Age predecessor state. Its nature — oracle, computation engine, imprisoned intelligence — is known only to the emperor. Some scholars believe Yelang's remarkable stability across centuries is not coincidence but the device's ongoing guidance.
