# Manthva (The Still Cities)

## §history
The Still Cities of Manthva predate living memory — and in a necrocracy, living memory spans millennia. The undead civilization arose on the shores and islands of the Gebed Mur, a vast freshwater sea roughly one hundred and fifty miles long and forty miles wide. How long ago the first dead chose to remain, to build, to govern rather than decay — that is a question even the eldest among them will not answer plainly. What is known is that Manthva's dead were present when much of the gallery system was constructed, and their accumulated intelligence about its architecture, its purposes, and its dangers is without equal in the known world. They watched the galleries being built. They remember the builders.

For centuries the Still Cities were governed by a single prince — most recently Prince Cai, whose rule was characterized by careful neutrality toward the living nations. Prince Cai was assassinated, and the princeship has sat vacant since. Three noble houses now compete for the throne: Hathli, Lucini, and Satlana. The assassination's architects have not been publicly identified, though whispers within the courts point toward one of the competing houses. The succession crisis has fractured Manthva's traditionally unified foreign posture, and each house now pursues its own diplomatic agenda — including overtures toward NYC.

## §geography
The Gebed Mur dominates Manthva's geography — a cold, dark freshwater sea that serves as both highway and boundary for the Still Cities. The cities themselves are built from ancient stone, rising from the shoreline and from islands within the sea. Black galleys move between them, their silhouettes visible from the Bronx ridgeline on clear days. The northern passes out of NYC reach the Bronx coast in four to six hours of travel, making Manthva the closest undead polity to the city. The water of the Gebed Mur is deep, cold, and unnervingly still — it does not freeze in winter, and fishermen from living settlements avoid it. The lakebed is said to hold drowned cities older than the current ones.

## §government
An aristocratic necrocracy. Political power is held by the undead nobility — ancient beings whose authority derives from age, accumulated knowledge, and the size of their undead retinues. The princeship, traditionally held by a single ruler chosen through consensus of the great houses, is currently vacant following Prince Cai's assassination. Three houses compete for the position: House Hathli, House Lucini, and House Satlana. Each commands its own undead forces, intelligence networks, and centuries of institutional memory. Governance operates on timescales incomprehensible to the living — a policy debate may span decades, a diplomatic initiative centuries. The living who dwell within Manthva's borders exist as subjects, servants, or commodities, depending on the house they serve.

## §culture
Manthvan culture is defined by temporal scale. The undead operate on timelines that render human urgency meaningless. Patience is not a virtue in the Still Cities — it is the fundamental condition of existence. The dead accumulate: knowledge, grudges, alliances, enemies, artifacts, secrets. A single Manthvan lord may hold personal memories spanning eight hundred to twelve hundred years, layered and cross-referenced in ways that make conversation with the living disorienting. A Manthvan noble discussing current events may reference a treaty signed six centuries ago as though it were yesterday, then struggle to distinguish between two human diplomats who visited thirty years apart — because to them, thirty years is nothing.

Art in Manthva tends toward the architectural and the archival. The dead build slowly and forever. Their cities are works of accumulated craft, each stone placed with the knowledge that it need never be replaced. Music exists but is rare — the dead prefer silence, and sound in the Still Cities carries an unsettling weight. The living who serve the houses often develop their own subcultures, but these are tolerated rather than respected.

## §sensory-palette
Black galleys on cold, still water, their oars moving in perfect unison without breath or exertion. Ancient stone cities lit by corpse-light — a pale, bluish-green luminescence that casts no warmth and flickers without wind. The smell of preservation unguents: myrrh, natron, cedar oil, and something older and sharper beneath. Cold stone under every surface, radiating a chill that seeps through boot leather. Silence — vast, architectural silence — interrupted by slow, deliberate speech in voices that have not needed to breathe for centuries. The faint creak of dried sinew when an undead lord turns to regard a visitor. Water lapping against stone quays with no tide and no current.

## §voice-notes
**House Hathli — Lord Cassivus (~800 years old):** Formal, measured cadences. Archaic diction — prefers constructions like "one might observe" and "it would be imprudent to disregard." Never raises his voice; displeasure manifests as increasingly precise grammar and longer pauses between clauses. Genuinely curious about NYC — asks detailed questions about governance, population, resource management. Speaks to the living as a scholar might speak to a promising but short-lived student.
*Example:* "One does not... offer cartography of the upper galleries... without considerable deliberation. You will understand, I trust, that such knowledge was old before your nation's founders drew breath."

**House Lucini — Lady Meridia (~1,200 years old):** Cold, analytical, openly contemptuous of the living. Views humans as structurally unreliable — their lifespans make them incapable of honoring commitments beyond a single generation. Speaks in clipped, efficient sentences. Rarely uses names; refers to living interlocutors by title or function. Does not perform warmth or courtesy.
*Example:* "You will be dead in forty years. Your successor will not remember this conversation. Why would I negotiate with you?"

**House Satlana — Lord Viccius (~600 years old):** Charismatic, affects warmth and informality. Laughs, gestures, uses colloquialisms. Calls visitors "friend" and "colleague." This is entirely calculated — Viccius understands that the living find him less threatening when he mimics their mannerisms. Beneath the performance, he is as patient and ruthless as any Manthvan lord. The youngest of the three house leaders and the most dangerous.
*Example:* "Look, I'll be honest with you — and I know that's unusual coming from one of us. Meridia will string you along for a century. Cassivus will bore you to dust. I'm offering you a deal that works for both of us, right now, today."

## §adventure-hooks
- The succession crisis presents an opportunity for NYC diplomacy: all three houses want something from the living, and each is willing to trade gallery intelligence for support. But backing the wrong house — or being seen to back any house — could unite the other two against NYC. A diplomatic mission to the Still Cities must navigate offers from all three houses without committing prematurely.
- House Hathli has offered gallery maps covering the upper levels in exchange for military support and a permanent embassy. The maps are genuine but deliberately incomplete — they omit everything below Level 5. Discovering this omission, and deciding how to respond, could reshape the alliance.
- Lady Meridia of House Lucini possesses the most complete gallery maps in existence, including partial intelligence about the throne-barrow. She will share this information only in exchange for knowledge about the Emperor's fragmentary consciousness — a subject NYC may or may not have intelligence on. Brokering this exchange without revealing too much is a delicate operation.
- Lord Viccius of House Satlana offers everything his rivals offer plus active military assistance and necromantic tutelage. His terms seem too good. Investigating Satlana's true agenda — including persistent rumors connecting the house to Prince Cai's assassination — could reveal whether Viccius is a valuable ally or the most dangerous actor in the succession crisis.

## §unknown
[UNKNOWN] — How long the Still Cities have truly existed. The eldest lords claim memories stretching back millennia, but the cities themselves show architectural strata suggesting construction phases that predate even the oldest undead currently active. Something — or someone — built the foundations of Manthva before the current necrocracy arose.

[UNKNOWN] — What intelligence House Lucini possesses about the throne-barrow. Lady Meridia references it obliquely, implying knowledge that would reshape understanding of the gallery system's deepest levels. She has never disclosed specifics, and no spy from the other houses has successfully extracted this information.

[UNKNOWN] — The full terms of House Satlana's "additional consideration." Lord Viccius's offers to NYC include provisions he describes only in private, face-to-face meetings with senior officials. Those who have heard the full terms do not discuss them afterward. Whether this reflects the sensitivity of the offer or something done to them during the meeting is unclear.
