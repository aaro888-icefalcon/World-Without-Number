# Corvath

## §geography
Corvath occupies a wind-scoured archipelago of black volcanic islands in the northern Ashenmere Sea. The climate is cold and wet, with near-constant fog banks rolling between the islands. Geothermal vents provide unexpected warmth in sheltered valleys, and the volcanic soil supports hardy root crops and grazing for shaggy highland cattle.

## §government
A maritime federation governed by the Tidesmoot, an assembly of island captains who convene twice yearly on the largest island. Each island maintains significant autonomy, with captains acting as both military commanders and civil administrators. Disputes between islands are settled by arbitration or, when that fails, by ritualized naval contests.

## §culture
Corvathi are a seafaring people who measure worth by seamanship, courage, and loyalty to crew. Oral tradition is sacred; their skalds memorize genealogies and histories stretching back centuries. Tattooing marks major life events, and a Corvathi elder's skin reads like a chronicle. Hospitality to travelers is absolute once they cross a threshold.

## §economy
Corvath's wealth comes from deep-sea fishing, whaling, and the harvesting of rare volcanic minerals prized by alchemists across the Latter Earth. Their shipwrights produce vessels renowned for resilience in heavy seas. Trade routes extend south to warmer nations, exchanging raw materials for grain, textiles, and luxuries the islands cannot produce.

## §military
The Corvathi navy is disproportionately powerful for the nation's size, crewing swift longships and heavier war-galleys hardened for northern waters. Every adult citizen can crew a vessel. There is no standing army; island militias assemble when needed, fighting with axes, harpoons, and a brutal efficiency born of lives spent battling the sea.

## §religion
The Corvathi venerate the Deepmother, a deity associated with the ocean's depths and volcanic fire beneath the seabed. Temples are built in sea caves where the tide rises and falls through the sanctuary. Priests read omens in the patterns of volcanic smoke and the movements of whale pods. The dead are given to the sea in weighted burial boats.

## §history
Corvath was settled by refugees fleeing a mainland catastrophe during the latter Fourth Age, though which catastrophe is disputed among the skalds. The islands were uninhabited but bore traces of a much older civilization beneath the volcanic rock. Three centuries ago, a unified Corvathi fleet broke a Dulimbi trade blockade, establishing the archipelago as an independent maritime power.

## §current-affairs
Corvath faces a crisis of succession. The last Tidesmoot ended without consensus on a new High Captain, and two rival factions now claim leadership.
