# Couront

## §history

Couront developed as a monastic kingdom, its governance shaped by the Bleeding God monasteries that served as centers of learning, agriculture, and spiritual authority long before the crown formalized secular rule. King Enguerrand's dynasty rules with the monasteries' blessing, and in practice the monks function as de facto local authorities across much of the territory. The kingdom has been chronically threatened by the Black Pact to its north, an existential pressure that has forged a culture of endurance, communal sacrifice, and theological resilience. NYC's arrival — millions of people ripped from their world and deposited in a hostile one — resonated deeply with Bleeding God theology, earning the displaced population a measure of theological respect rare among Amundi kingdoms.

## §geography

Couront occupies cool, densely forested territory with a population of approximately 600,000. Monasteries are scattered throughout the forests, connected by footpaths and pilgrimage routes rather than major roads. The capital is modest by Amundi standards, serving primarily as a seat for King Enguerrand's court and a mustering point for defense against Black Pact incursions. The kingdom borders Hammersea trade routes to the north and Black Pact territories beyond. Monastery compounds — stone cloisters, herb gardens, timber outbuildings — dot the landscape at regular intervals, each serving as a local hub of governance, healing, and food production.

## §government

King Enguerrand rules a modest but stable kingdom in partnership with the Bleeding God monasteries. Secular authority handles defense, taxation, and external diplomacy; monastic authority governs local justice, education, healing, and spiritual life. This dual structure functions smoothly because both crown and monasteries share the same existential threat from the Black Pact. The military is small but experienced in defensive woodland warfare. Couront is cautiously supportive of NYC, providing modest food aid and local knowledge without overextending its limited resources.

## §culture

Courontian culture centers on the Bleeding God's theology of suffering, endurance, and service. Hardship is not merely tolerated but valued as spiritually meaningful. Monks in rough-spun robes are the most respected figures in society, and monastery life — daily labor, communal meals, contemplative silence — sets the cultural rhythm even for secular communities. Hospitality is offered freely to travelers, especially those in genuine need. The monasteries serve as safe houses for travelers heading into the far north, providing supplies, guidance, and shelter from Black Pact raiding parties.

## §sensory-palette

- Dense cool forests where sunlight filters through canopy in shifting green-gold patterns
- Monastery bells ringing at dawn, midday, and dusk, their sound carrying through still forest air
- Stone cloisters worn smooth by centuries of sandaled feet, moss growing in the joints
- Monks in rough-spun robes tending herb gardens, the smell of lavender, rosemary, and medicinal compounds
- King Enguerrand's modest court: functional stone, practical furnishings, maps of the northern border prominent on every wall
- Forest paths between monasteries, marked by carved waystone pillars depicting the Bleeding God's suffering

## §voice-notes

Measured, compassionate, theologically inflected. Courontians reference suffering as virtue without self-pity — it is simply how the world works and how the faithful respond. Monks speak slowly, choosing words with care born from contemplative practice. King Enguerrand is pragmatic and direct, his speech reflecting military experience tempered by genuine faith. Common people are quiet, hospitable, and stoic. Theological language permeates everyday speech: "The God bleeds with us" is a standard expression of solidarity.

## §adventure-hooks

- A monastery near the Black Pact border sends urgent word that raiding parties have intensified and requests NYC assistance in exchange for maps and intelligence about northern territories
- Courontian monks offer to share their knowledge of local medicinal plants and sustainable farming techniques — invaluable for NYC's survival — but require NYC representatives to undertake a pilgrimage to understand the land's spiritual character first
- King Enguerrand proposes using the monastery network as a covert communication and supply chain for NYC operations in the far north, but the arrangement requires NYC to help defend monastery outposts
- A senior monk approaches NYC with disturbing questions about the Bleeding God's true nature, suggesting the monasteries' power may derive from something other than divine grace, and requesting NYC's outsider perspective
