# Vois

## §history

Vois emerged as a republic centuries ago when its merchant and artisan classes overthrew a tyrannical duke and established democratic governance through a constitutional convention. The republic has endured through careful institutional design, rule of law, and a commercial culture that values stability over expansion. Vois has weathered Amundi conflicts through neutrality, trade diplomacy, and a citizen militia capable enough to make invasion costly without being threatening enough to provoke one. The arrival of NYC presented Vois with a philosophical mirror — another democratic society — and a practical dilemma: sympathy without the resources or political will to offer meaningful aid.

## §geography

Vois occupies a compact, defensible territory of rolling farmland, merchant towns, and well-maintained roads. The population of approximately 800,000 is concentrated in orderly urban centers connected by trade routes to neighboring kingdoms. The capital features public forums, a legislative assembly hall, and merchant guild headquarters arranged around a central civic square. The countryside is productive and well-managed, reflecting the republic's institutional competence. Borders are clearly demarcated and patrolled by citizen militia.

## §government

Democratic governance operates through elected assemblies, independent courts, and constitutional constraints on executive power. Representatives serve fixed terms. Laws are codified, publicly posted, and enforced with procedural consistency. Commercial regulation is thorough — contracts are sacred, disputes adjudicated by specialized trade courts. This system produces competent governance but also institutional caution: every foreign policy decision requires debate, committee review, and majority approval. Emergency action is constitutionally difficult by design.

## §culture

Voisian culture prizes measured discourse, legal precision, commercial integrity, and civic participation. Citizens take pride in their republic and view monarchies and despotisms as primitive governance forms. Trade is conducted on strict, fair terms — no charity, no exploitation, no exceptions. Public education is widespread. The arts flourish within commercial patronage frameworks. Voisians are polite, informed, and frustratingly deliberate in their decision-making. They are the Amundi kingdom most culturally similar to NYC, which makes their cautious unwillingness to help more painful than outright hostility would be.

## §sensory-palette

- Clean stone streets swept daily by civic work crews, drainage channels functioning properly
- Public forums where citizens debate policy in orderly turns, voices carrying across stone amphitheaters
- Merchant houses with glass windows, ledger books visible on desks, scales calibrated and certified
- Orderly marketplaces with posted prices, standardized weights, and trade court officials circulating
- The legislative assembly hall: tiered wooden benches, a speaker's rostrum, sunlight through clerestory windows

## §voice-notes

Measured, legalistic, polite but firm. Voisians choose words with care and expect the same from others. Arguments are structured — premise, evidence, conclusion. Emotional appeals are considered poor form. "With respect" is a genuine qualifier, not a passive-aggressive preamble. Merchants quote contract clauses from memory. Politicians speak in complete paragraphs. Casual conversation exists but tends toward the informatively pleasant rather than the warmly personal.

## §adventure-hooks

- Vois offers NYC a trade agreement with fair terms but strict conditions that limit NYC's ability to negotiate with Pelegrin — accepting means commercial security at the cost of diplomatic flexibility
- A Voisian legislator privately sympathetic to NYC proposes an aid package that requires NYC to adopt Voisian legal standards — a reasonable ask that would consume months of institutional effort NYC cannot spare
- Voisian intelligence services make quiet contact with NYC, offering to share information about Pelegrinian military movements in exchange for access to NYC's unique technological knowledge
- A faction within the Voisian assembly argues that NYC's democratic experiment must be supported as a matter of republican principle — but they lack the votes, and need NYC's help building their political case
