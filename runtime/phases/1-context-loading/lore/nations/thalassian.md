# Thalassian

## §history
The Thalassian Confederation is a maritime nation comprising hundreds of islands scattered across the eastern Sunward Sea. Its people are the Latter Earth's greatest sailors, navigating by star, current, and an oral tradition of wave-reading passed down for generations. The confederation was formalized a century ago when the island chiefs agreed to mutual defense against mainland empires that coveted their strategic position. Before that, the Thalassians had sailed these waters since time beyond memory, claiming descent from a Second Age seafaring civilization.

## §geography
Hundreds of islands ranging from tiny coral atolls to large volcanic landmasses. The waters are warm, clear, and treacherous — coral reefs, unpredictable currents, and sudden squalls make navigation perilous for outsiders. The larger islands support tropical forests, freshwater springs, and agriculture. The smaller ones are little more than sand and palm trees. Underwater Legacy ruins are visible in the shallows around many islands.

## §government
A confederation of island chiefs who meet annually at the Great Reef — a natural amphitheater of coral. Each chief governs their island or island group autonomously. Confederal decisions require consensus. A Speaker of the Tides is elected to represent the confederation externally. Naval defense is coordinated through a network of signal fires and messenger canoes.

## §culture
Thalassian culture is built around the sea. Boats are sacred objects, constructed with rituals as important as the carpentry. Navigation skill is the highest social currency. Oral storytelling preserves history and law. Tattoos mark lineage, achievements, and spiritual status. Communal feasts of seafood and tropical fruit accompany every significant occasion. The Thalassians are warm, generous, and absolutely lethal to those who threaten their waters.

## §cultural-implications
Sailing, swimming, and navigation skill checks earn deep respect. Thalassian NPCs are welcoming but observe strangers carefully. Disrespecting the sea — littering, boasting about controlling it — provokes anger. Sharing food and drink is essential for social bonding. Landlocked PCs may be viewed with friendly amusement.

## §sensory-palette
Thalassia is turquoise water and white sand under an infinite blue sky. The air tastes of salt and coconut. Outrigger canoes with painted sails dot the channels between islands. At night, bioluminescent plankton light the shallows with ethereal blue-green fire. The rhythm of drums and chanting carries across the water from island to island.

## §voice-notes
Thalassians speak with a rolling, wave-like cadence, drawing out vowels. They use sea metaphors for everything: "That plan has a strong keel" and "Don't sail into a lee shore." Common greeting: "Fair winds." They sing as much as they speak — daily activities are accompanied by work songs.

## §adventure-hooks
- A sacred island has risen from the depths overnight, covered in Legacy structures never seen before. The chiefs cannot agree on whether to explore it or declare it taboo.
- Thalassian fishermen are pulling up artifacts from the deep — not from known ruins, but from somewhere farther down. The artifacts are warm to the touch and hum faintly.
- A mainland fleet is approaching Thalassian waters with unclear intentions. The confederation must decide whether to negotiate, flee, or fight — and they must decide quickly.
- The eldest navigator claims the stars have shifted position. Other navigators confirm it. Traditional routes are becoming unreliable, and the cause is unknown.

## §unknown
[UNKNOWN] — The Thalassians' claim of Second Age descent is supported by evidence they do not share with outsiders. Certain sacred islands contain structures that match no known Legacy architecture — they are older, built from materials that do not corrode, and inscribed with symbols that predate all known writing systems. What these structures are, and what connection the Thalassians truly have to them, is their most closely guarded secret.
