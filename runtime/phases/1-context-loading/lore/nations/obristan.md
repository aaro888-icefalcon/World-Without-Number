# Obristan

## §history
Obristan is a landlocked crossroads nation that has survived by making itself indispensable to everyone. Situated at the junction of major trade routes, it has perfected the art of neutrality. Founded by a coalition of merchant families who bought their independence from a collapsing empire, Obristan has maintained its sovereignty through diplomacy, bribery, and the simple economic argument that conquering it would disrupt trade for everyone. It hosts the only internationally recognized neutral ground for diplomatic negotiations.

## §geography
A broad river valley at the confluence of three major trade routes, surrounded by low hills. The terrain is unremarkable — flat farmland and gentle woodlands. The climate is mild and continental. The capital, Obris, sits at the meeting point of the Greywater and the Blackflow rivers. The land is densely settled and intensively farmed.

## §government
A merchant oligarchy governed by the Trade Council — representatives of the twelve wealthiest trading houses. The Council elects a Consul to handle day-to-day governance. Foreign policy is managed by the Office of Neutrality, a specialized diplomatic corps renowned for its skill. The military is small but well-trained, focused entirely on defense.

## §culture
Obristani culture is pragmatic, cosmopolitan, and aggressively non-confrontational. Every culture is represented in its markets, and cultural tolerance is both genuine and economically motivated. The arts favor music and theater that draw from multiple traditions. Food is a fusion of a dozen cuisines. Conflict resolution is prized — Obristanis make excellent mediators and terrible warriors.

## §cultural-implications
Diplomacy and commerce checks are at their most effective here. Obristani NPCs avoid taking sides and expect the same of visitors. Violence is met with universal condemnation and swift legal consequences. Information brokerage is a respected profession — everyone trades in secrets here.

## §sensory-palette
Obris is a cacophony of languages, a kaleidoscope of foreign dress and customs. The great bazaar stretches for miles, its canopied stalls selling goods from every nation. The air is thick with competing spices, grilling meats, and exotic perfumes. River barges crowd the waterways. The sound of haggling never stops, even at night.

## §voice-notes
Obristanis speak whatever language their audience prefers, switching mid-sentence if needed. They are relentlessly agreeable: "Of course, of course" and "Let us find a solution that benefits all parties." They avoid strong statements, hedge their opinions, and always leave themselves room to negotiate. Direct questions receive indirect answers.

## §adventure-hooks
- Two nations are on the brink of war, and Obristan is hosting emergency peace talks. Someone is sabotaging the negotiations — assassinating delegates and planting false evidence.
- A trading house has been secretly selling weapons to both sides of a foreign conflict. The evidence is damning, but exposing it would destabilize Obristan's carefully balanced politics.
- The Office of Neutrality has intercepted communications suggesting a foreign power plans to annex Obristan. The Consul needs proof that will convince all twelve trading houses to act in unison.
- A Legacy artifact of immense power has surfaced in the bazaar. Three nations have claimed ownership. Obristan must adjudicate before the dispute turns violent.

## §unknown
[UNKNOWN] — Obristan's founding charter contains a clause written in a language no living scholar can read. The merchant families treat it as a binding obligation, performing a specific ritual at the confluence of the rivers every equinox. They will not discuss what the clause says or what the ritual does. Disrupting it, they insist, would mean the end of Obristan.
