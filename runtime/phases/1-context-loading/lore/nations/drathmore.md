# Drathmore

## §history
Drathmore is a haunted kingdom — literally. The land is saturated with necromantic energy from a Third Age burial complex of staggering size. Unlike Ithkarn, where the undead rule, Drathmore's living inhabitants struggle to maintain sovereignty over a land where the dead rise uninvited. The kingdom was founded by the Order of the Final Rest, a militant religious order dedicated to putting the dead to permanent rest. After a century of effort, they have achieved an uneasy equilibrium — the dead still rise, but the Order keeps them contained.

## §geography
Rolling moorland under perpetually overcast skies. The soil is dark and loamy, dotted with ancient barrows, standing stones, and the entrances to the vast burial complex below. Fog is common. Rain is frequent. The temperature is cool year-round. Patches of dead vegetation mark areas of intense necromantic concentration. The few forests are stunted and twisted.

## §government
A theocratic military order. The Grand Commander of the Final Rest holds executive authority. Knight-Commanders govern provinces. Civilian administration is handled by a separate bureaucracy, but military priorities always supersede civilian concerns. All citizens serve in the militia and are trained in basic anti-undead protocols.

## §culture
Drathmorans are grim, devout, and disciplined. Their faith — the Doctrine of Final Rest — holds that death should be permanent and that the unquiet dead are an abomination. Daily life revolves around protective rituals, community patrols, and the grim business of putting down the risen. Art is solemn — hymns, memorial carvings, and illuminated manuscripts recording the names of the finally rested. Humor exists but is dry and centered on death.

## §cultural-implications
Religion, undead lore, and combat skill checks are valued. Drathmore NPCs respect piety and martial discipline. Necromancy is the ultimate taboo — even discussing it academically provokes hostility. PCs who help fight the undead earn immediate trust. Those who dabble in death magic are expelled or worse.

## §sensory-palette
Drathmore is grey and somber. Fog clings to the moors like a shroud. The air smells of damp earth, cold stone, and the faint sweetness of decay that never quite leaves. Church bells toll at regular intervals — each pattern carries meaning, from "all clear" to "rising in sector seven." The silence between the bells is heavy with anticipation.

## §voice-notes
Drathmorans speak in measured, solemn tones. They use religious language casually: "Rest grant you peace" (greeting) and "May your rest be final" (farewell). Common expressions: "The dead do not forgive" and "Vigilance unto the last." They do not joke about death — everything else is fair game.

## §adventure-hooks
- The burial complex has shifted — tunnels that were sealed have opened, and sealed chambers are accessible for the first time. The undead activity has spiked dramatically.
- A Knight-Commander has gone missing during a routine patrol of the deep barrows. Her squad was found dead — but at rest, not risen. Something down there killed them cleanly.
- A scholar has arrived claiming that the burial complex is not a cemetery but a prison, and that the Order's methods of "putting to rest" are not destroying the undead but driving them deeper.
- Children born in Drathmore are increasingly manifesting sensitivity to necromantic energy — seeing the dead before they rise, hearing whispers from the barrows. The Order does not know whether to train them or fear them.

## §unknown
[UNKNOWN] — The burial complex extends far deeper than the Order has explored. At the deepest mapped level, the architecture changes — from burial chambers to something resembling a throne room. A door at its far end is sealed with methods the Order cannot breach. Something behind it is aware, patient, and occasionally speaks to those who listen too long.
