# Mishar

## §history

Mishar rose to prominence as the Arena Kingdom, forging its identity around spectacle, competition, and the transactional nature of power. Under a succession of despots, Jilat grew from a walled frontier town into a fortified city-state whose great arena became the cultural and political heart of the nation. Despot Amiya, the current ruler, seized power through a combination of personal combat prowess and political cunning, eliminating rivals in contests both literal and figurative. Mishar is the nearest Amundi kingdom to NYC and was among the first to make contact with the displaced population.

## §geography

Mishar occupies the borderlands closest to NYC's arrival point, bounded by Pelegrin to the east. The capital Jilat sits behind mighty walls, its skyline dominated by the great arena at the city center. The surrounding countryside supports a population of approximately 1.5 million through mixed agriculture and livestock, sufficient to feed hundreds of thousands but not millions. Trade roads radiate from Jilat to border markets and neighboring territories.

## §government

Despot Amiya rules with theatrical authority and personal menace. Charismatic, calculating, and personally dangerous in combat, Amiya governs through a system where everything is a transaction. Champions who win favor in the arena gain political influence; losers are fed to the dogs. The military comprises roughly 5,000 standing soldiers supplemented by levy forces. Amiya views NYC as a resource to be exploited, not a partner to be cultivated.

## §culture

Arena culture pervades every level of Misharan society. Common speech is thick with arena metaphors — negotiations are "bouts," agreements are "victories," and failures are "falls." Respect is earned exclusively through displays of prowess, whether martial, mercantile, or rhetorical. The marketplace operates on aggressive bartering, and even minor social encounters carry an undercurrent of competitive display.

## §sensory-palette

- The great arena at Jilat's center: tiered stone seats, sand-floored pit, iron gates for beast and champion alike
- Roaring crowds whose cheers echo off the mighty walls
- Dust and blood hanging in the air after a bout
- The mighty walls of Jilat, scarred by siege and weather, draped with banners of past champions
- Marketplace bartering — raised voices, clinking coin, the smell of roasting meat from vendor stalls
- Amiya's throne room: trophies of defeated rivals, weapon racks doubling as decoration

## §voice-notes

Amiya speaks with theatrical authority, every sentence calibrated for effect. Pronouncements carry the cadence of an arena announcer declaring a match. Common Misharans adopt this pattern — direct, transactional, laced with competitive metaphor. "What do you bring to the sand?" is a standard greeting. Flattery is tolerated only when backed by demonstrated capability. Weakness is not hated but dismissed.

## §adventure-hooks

- Amiya demands NYC send champions to the arena as a condition of trade negotiations — refusal is an insult, but participation risks lives
- A Misharan merchant offers bulk grain at reasonable prices, but the contract includes a clause requiring NYC laborers to serve in Jilat's border fortifications against Pelegrin
- An arena champion from Jilat defects to NYC claiming knowledge of Amiya's spy network — Mishar demands their return
- Pelegrin forces probe Mishar's eastern border, and Amiya proposes a mutual defense pact with NYC that would formalize NYC's subordinate status
