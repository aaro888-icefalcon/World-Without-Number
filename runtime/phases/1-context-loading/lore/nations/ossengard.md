# Ossengard

## §history
Ossengard is a city of bone. Built on and from the skeleton of a creature so vast that its ribcage forms the city's main district, Ossengard began as a curiosity and became a nation. The creature — called the Colossus by locals — died in a prior age, its body petrified into a material harder than stone. Settlers discovered that the bone-material could be carved and shaped, and that it possessed unusual properties: it dampened magic, repelled certain monsters, and maintained a constant temperature. Ossengard grew within and around the Colossus's remains, becoming a city unlike any other.

## §geography
The Colossus lies on a coastal plain, its skeleton stretching over two miles from skull to tail. The ribcage district — the main city — is sheltered beneath enormous curved ribs that arch overhead like the vaults of a cathedral. Other districts occupy the skull, the pelvis, and structures built along the spine. The surrounding plain is flat grassland. The coast is nearby, providing fishing and trade access. The climate is temperate, moderated by the bone-material's temperature regulation.

## §government
A guild republic. The Bone-Carvers' Guild — master craftsmen who work the Colossus's material — hold primary political power. A council of guild representatives governs. The Anatomist — a scholar who studies the Colossus itself — serves as advisor to the council. Citizenship is open; political participation requires guild membership.

## §culture
Ossengardian culture is defined by its extraordinary setting. Architecture is organic — carved from or built onto the Colossus's bones, producing buildings of swooping, natural curves. Art favors sculpture (the medium is irresistible) and a unique form of music that uses the Colossus's hollow bones as resonating chambers. Food is coastal and plain — fish, bread, dairy. The Colossus is treated with a mixture of reverence and pragmatism: it is their home, their livelihood, and a constant reminder that things of unimaginable scale once walked the earth.

## §cultural-implications
Crafting, history, and anatomy skill checks are valued. Ossengard NPCs are proud of their city and eager to explain its wonders. Knowledge of Legacy creatures or prior-age biology earns special respect. The magic-dampening property of the bone-material means magical PCs may find their abilities weakened within the city — this is a known trade-off, not hostility.

## §sensory-palette
Ossengard is surreal. Enormous petrified ribs arch overhead, casting curved shadows on streets carved from vertebrae. The bone-material is pale ivory, warm to the touch, and faintly resonant — footsteps produce a deep, subtle hum. The air smells of sea salt and the dry, mineral scent of ancient bone. Music played within the rib-vaults produces acoustics found nowhere else — hauntingly beautiful and slightly unnerving.

## §voice-notes
Ossengardians speak with an easy, proud cadence. They use anatomical metaphors: "That plan has a spine" and "Don't get under the ribs of that problem." Common greeting: "Welcome to the bones." They are enthusiastic about explaining their city to newcomers and mildly offended by those who find it macabre. They refer to the Colossus as "the Old One" with genuine affection.

## §adventure-hooks
- Workers carving a new district in the Colossus's tail have found something inside the bone — a chamber, sealed and pressurized, containing a fluid that is not fossilized. Something inside the Colossus may still be alive.
- The magic-dampening field has intensified sharply in certain areas, creating zones where no magic functions at all. The zones are expanding. The Anatomist believes the Colossus is reacting to something.
- A visiting scholar has identified markings on the Colossus's skull that match Sorcerer-King combat inscriptions. The Colossus may not have died naturally — it was killed. The weapon that killed it may still be nearby.
- A second skeleton has been discovered beneath the seabed, just offshore. It is larger than the first. And unlike the Colossus, its bone-material is not entirely petrified.

## §unknown
[UNKNOWN] — The Colossus was not an animal. Anatomical analysis reveals structures that have no biological analog — chambers within the bones that resemble machinery, channels that may have carried energy rather than blood, and a skull structure suggesting intelligence of a high order. Whether the Colossus was engineered, evolved, or something else entirely — and whether others of its kind exist — is the question that drives the Anatomist's research and haunts Ossengard's dreams.
