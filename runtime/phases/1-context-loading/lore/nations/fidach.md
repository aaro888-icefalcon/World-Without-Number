# Fidach

## §history

Fidach has never been unified under a single ruler and regards this as a point of pride. The highland clans have warred, feasted, and traded with each other since before recorded Amundi history, resisting every attempt at external conquest through sheer stubbornness and terrain advantage. The only settlement large enough to be called a city is Kennet, the Hammersea port, which has been burnt to the ground at least a dozen times by raiding parties, internal feuds, and foreign incursions — and rebuilt each time, rougher and more defiant than before.

## §geography

Fidach occupies windswept highland moors and rugged uplands, cut by steep valleys and fast rivers. The terrain is poor for large-scale agriculture but supports hardy livestock and scattered crofting communities. Kennet sits on the Hammersea coast, providing access to the vital Hammersea trade route and the northern diplomatic landscape beyond. The highlands themselves are difficult to traverse for outsiders unfamiliar with clan paths and weather patterns. Population is approximately 500,000, dispersed across clan territories.

## §government

There is no central ruler. Authority rests with clan chiefs, whose power derives from personal martial prowess and the loyalty of their warriors. Disputes are settled through single combat, raids, or seasonal moots where chiefs gather to drink, boast, and occasionally negotiate. Alliances form and dissolve based on personal relationships and feuds that can stretch back generations. Kennet operates under a loose council of whichever clans currently control the port district.

## §culture

Respect in Fidach is earned through martial prowess — there is no other currency that matters. Hospitality is sacred but conditional: a guest who cannot defend their honor is pitied, not welcomed. Clan halls are the center of social life, filled with smoke from open hearths, strong drink, and louder stories. Boasting is expected and ritualized; a warrior who cannot tell the tale of their deeds is considered incomplete. Fidachans view southern Amundi kingdoms as soft and overly complicated.

## §sensory-palette

- Windswept highland moors under grey skies, heather and bracken stretching to the horizon
- Smoky clan halls — timber and stone, animal hides on walls, fire pits crackling with peat
- Strong drink passed in wooden cups, the burn of highland spirits
- Rain — constant, driving, horizontal — soaking everything and everyone
- Kennet's harbor: salt air, creaking timber ships, the shouts of dockworkers rebuilding what last burned
- The clash of iron and the roar of challenge at clan moots

## §voice-notes

Blunt, direct speech with no patience for diplomatic circumlocution. A challenge is a greeting; asking "Can you fight?" is considered polite interest. Boasting is expected and must be backed up or retracted publicly. Fidachans drop formality entirely with those they respect and become monosyllabic with those they do not. Humor is dry, dark, and delivered without smiling.

## §adventure-hooks

- A Fidachan clan chief offers NYC access to the Hammersea trade route in exchange for warriors to settle a blood feud — NYC must decide whether to entangle itself in clan politics
- Kennet burns again, this time under suspicious circumstances, and refugees flood toward NYC territory seeking temporary shelter
- A Fidachan raiding party mistakes NYC scouts for rival clansmen, creating a diplomatic incident that can only be resolved through the highland custom of single combat
- Rumors reach NYC of something ancient stirring beneath the Hammersea, and Fidachan fishermen are disappearing from their boats
