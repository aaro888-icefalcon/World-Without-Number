# Montaigne

## §history
Montaigne is the cultural heart of the western nations — a kingdom where art, philosophy, and courtly intrigue are elevated to supreme importance. Founded two centuries ago when a charismatic duke unified the western provinces through marriage alliances and cultural patronage rather than warfare, Montaigne has since set the standard for civilization in its region. Its court is the most sophisticated — and most treacherous — in the Latter Earth.

## §geography
Fertile lowlands with gentle rivers, ancient forests, and vineyard-covered hillsides. The climate is warm and pleasant, with mild winters and long summers. The countryside is picturesque and extensively cultivated. Major cities are connected by well-maintained roads — a point of national pride. The western coast faces the Sunward Sea.

## §government
An absolute monarchy tempered by the influence of a powerful aristocracy. The king rules from the Palace of Mirrors in the capital, surrounded by a court of nobles who compete for royal favor through wit, artistic patronage, and Byzantine political maneuvering. Real governance happens in salon conversations and private audiences, not in formal councils.

## §culture
Montaignard culture prizes elegance, wit, and artistic refinement above all. Fashion is an obsession. Conversation is an art form — a well-turned phrase can make a reputation, and a clumsy one can destroy it. Dueling over matters of honor is legal and common among the aristocracy. The cuisine is legendary. Beneath the glittering surface, ruthless ambition drives every interaction.

## §cultural-implications
Charisma and social skill checks are paramount. Montaignard NPCs judge by appearance, manners, and verbal sophistication. Crude or direct behavior marks one as a provincial. Knowledge of art, wine, and courtly etiquette provides significant social advantages. The court is a maze of factions — choosing allies carefully is essential.

## §sensory-palette
Montaigne is perfumed — literally. The air in its cities carries notes of lavender, rose water, and expensive cologne. Music spills from every open window: harpsichords, viols, trained voices. The architecture is ornate, all carved stone facades and gilded ironwork. Even the common markets are arranged with an eye for beauty.

## §voice-notes
Montaignards speak in elaborate, flowing sentences with frequent parenthetical asides. They use understatement and irony as primary tools. Common expressions: "How delightfully provincial" (an insult) and "One does what one must" (a justification for anything). They never say directly what can be implied.

## §adventure-hooks
- A prominent artist has been murdered, and the painting she was working on has vanished. The subject of the painting — a powerful duke — is the obvious suspect, but the truth involves secrets far more dangerous than scandal.
- The king's heir has disappeared during a masked ball. Ransom demands have not materialized, suggesting the kidnapping has political rather than financial motives.
- A series of duels has decimated the officer corps of the royal guard. Investigation reveals the duels were provoked by a single individual using disguises and forged insults.
- An ancient wine cellar beneath the Palace of Mirrors has been breached during renovations, revealing tunnels that predate the palace by millennia.

## §unknown
[UNKNOWN] — The Palace of Mirrors was built on the site of a Third Age structure. The mirrors in the palace's famous Hall of Reflection are not Montaignard craftsmanship — they are Legacy artifacts, original to the site. On certain nights, courtiers report seeing reflections that do not match reality. The king is aware of this and has posted guards who answer only to him.
