# Xanthia

## §history
Xanthia is a decadent city-state built entirely within and upon a colossal Legacy structure — a golden-hued arcology that the locals call the Hive. The original settlers discovered the Hive three centuries ago and found it partially functional: climate-controlled, self-repairing, and producing a steady supply of a nutrient paste that, while bland, sustains life. Freed from the necessities of agriculture and construction, Xanthian society turned inward, developing elaborate arts, complex social hierarchies, and an obsessive culture of pleasure and intrigue.

## §geography
The Hive is a single structure roughly three miles across and half a mile tall, situated in an otherwise barren rocky desert. Its golden exterior resists all attempts at damage or modification. Interior spaces range from vast atriums to intimate chambers, connected by corridors, lifts, and passages that occasionally rearrange themselves. The surrounding desert is lifeless — the Hive is an island of habitation in a wasteland.

## §government
An aristocracy of aesthetes. The ruling class — the Gilded — are families who control access to the Hive's most desirable living spaces and its functional Legacy systems. A rotating council of Gilded families governs, primarily concerned with maintaining social order and controlling access to the Hive's deeper levels. Real power belongs to whoever controls the most functional Legacy systems.

## §culture
Xanthian culture is hedonistic, sophisticated, and profoundly insular. With material needs met by the Hive, Xanthians devote themselves to art, fashion, social competition, and the pursuit of novel experiences. Beauty is worshipped. Boredom is the greatest sin. The arts are avant-garde to the point of incomprehensibility. Outsiders are exotic curiosities — welcomed, displayed, and discarded when their novelty fades.

## §cultural-implications
Performance, persuasion, and artistic skill checks are valued. Xanthian NPCs are easily bored and constantly seeking novelty. PCs who are unusual or entertaining gain access; those who are ordinary are ignored. The Gilded are capricious patrons — generous one moment, cruel the next. Navigating Xanthian society requires reading social currents that shift by the hour.

## §sensory-palette
The Hive's interior is warm, golden, and faintly humming. Bioluminescent panels cast soft amber light. The air is temperature-controlled and carries faint floral scents. Music plays constantly — live performers compete with Legacy sound systems that emit tones on the edge of hearing. Every surface is decorated, draped, or repurposed as art. The effect is overwhelming and slightly claustrophobic.

## §voice-notes
Xanthians speak in languid, affected tones, drawing out syllables for dramatic effect. They use superlatives constantly: "utterly divine," "exquisitely dreadful." Common expressions: "How deliciously novel" and "One simply must experience it." They change topics rapidly and consider sustained focus on a single subject tedious.

## §adventure-hooks
- The Hive's self-repair systems have begun modifying inhabited areas — walls moving, rooms merging, corridors sealing. The changes follow a pattern no one can decipher.
- A Gilded family has discovered a new level deep within the Hive. They are keeping it secret, but rumors of miraculous discoveries are spreading. Other families are hiring outsiders to investigate.
- The nutrient paste that feeds the city has changed flavor and color. Chemical analysis shows new compounds that no one can identify. The Gilded insist everything is fine. The lower classes are not convinced.
- An outsider artist has become the toast of Xanthian society, but her art is having strange effects on viewers — obsession, hallucination, personality changes. She claims the Hive is creating through her.

## §unknown
[UNKNOWN] — The Hive is not a building. Xanthia's most knowledgeable technicians suspect it is a single organism — or a machine so complex that the distinction is meaningless. Its self-repair systems, environmental controls, and nutrient production suggest purpose. Whether the Hive was designed to house people, whether it is aware of its inhabitants, and what it might eventually do with them are questions the Gilded prefer not to ask.
