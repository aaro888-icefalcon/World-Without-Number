# Frostholme

## §history
Frostholme is a dwarven hold — one of the last great underground cities, carved into the heart of the Pale Heights during the Third Age. When the Shattering struck, the dwarves sealed their gates and waited. They emerged a century later to find the world changed but their city intact. Frostholme has maintained continuous occupation since, its deep forges and mining operations providing metals and manufactured goods that no surface nation can match. Relations with surface nations are commercial and cautious.

## §geography
The city exists primarily underground, carved into a mountain in the Pale Heights. Surface installations are limited to fortified gate-towns, watchtowers, and trading posts. The interior is vast — great halls, residential warrens, forges, farms lit by Legacy crystal arrays, and mines extending miles into the earth. The surface climate is subarctic; underground, Legacy heating systems maintain comfortable temperatures. Deep passages connect to unexplored cavern systems.

## §government
A council of Guild Masters, each representing a major craft or trade. The High Smith — elected from among the Guild Masters — serves as executive and chief judge. Governance is conservative, meritocratic, and focused on maintaining the city's infrastructure. Citizenship requires guild membership. Political change is measured in decades.

## §culture
Frostholmi culture values craftsmanship, endurance, and collective responsibility. Every dwarf is trained in a craft from childhood. Quality of work defines social standing. Oaths are binding — breaking one is the gravest crime. Art is inseparable from craft: a well-made tool is art, a beautiful but non-functional object is waste. Music features deep choral harmonics that resonate through the stone halls. Food is hearty — mushroom-based, supplemented by subterranean fish and surface trade.

## §cultural-implications
Crafting and engineering skill checks earn deep respect. Frostholmi NPCs judge outsiders by the quality of their equipment and the calluses on their hands. Boasting without proof invites challenge. Oaths made to or in the presence of Frostholmi are expected to be honored. PCs who demonstrate practical skill and keep their word build trust quickly.

## §sensory-palette
Frostholme is warmth within cold stone — the orange glow of forges, the ring of hammers on metal echoing through cavernous halls, the smell of hot iron and stone dust. Crystal arrays cast steady white light over broad avenues carved from living rock. Water runs in stone channels alongside every thoroughfare. The deep hum of the mountain's own resonance is audible in quiet moments.

## §voice-notes
Frostholmi speak in a low, deliberate register with emphasis on consonants. They use craft metaphors: "That plan needs more tempering" and "A weak weld fails under load." Common greeting: "Strong stone beneath you." They say little and mean everything. Humor is dry and delivered without expression.

## §adventure-hooks
- A deep mining expedition has broken through into a cavern system containing ruins of a civilization older than the dwarves themselves. The architecture is wrong — too large, too alien. Something lives there still.
- Legacy heating systems in the city's lower levels are failing. Temperatures are dropping, and the guild engineers cannot identify the cause. The problem may lie in sections of the city that were sealed and abandoned centuries ago.
- A surface nation is attempting to purchase exclusive rights to Frostholme's metal output, offering an extraordinary price. The deal would make Frostholme wealthy but dependent. The Guild Council is divided.
- A young smith has produced an alloy that replicates Legacy metal properties. The process is revolutionary — and it requires a material found only in the unexplored deep caverns where miners have been disappearing.

## §unknown
[UNKNOWN] — The dwarves did not carve Frostholme. They found it — empty, intact, and clearly built for inhabitants of their stature. The official history claims the city was carved by Third Age dwarven ancestors, but Guild Master records — accessible only to sitting Masters — tell a different story. Who or what built Frostholme, and why it was abandoned before the dwarves arrived, is the city's most closely guarded secret.
