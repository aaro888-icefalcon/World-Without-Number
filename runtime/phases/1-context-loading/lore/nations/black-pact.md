# The Black Pact

## §history

The Black Pact is not a single entity but a tradition — a 3,000-year lineage of sorcerous schools that bind themselves to extra-Iteral patrons in exchange for power. Over that span, thirty-six or more schools have risen, flourished, and fallen. Six survive today. The fallen schools leave behind school-cities — fortress-academies that become dungeons, their corridors still protected by affiliation-triggered wards keyed to patronal signatures. These ruins are immensely valuable repositories of magical knowledge, artifacts, and material wealth. NYC's magically un-affiliated population can enter these ruins without triggering the wards — a unique economic niche of enormous value that makes NYC a strategic asset to surviving schools.

## §geography

Northwestern territories beyond Couront. Cold, mountainous terrain cut by deep valleys and wind-scoured plateaus. Each surviving school-city is a fortress-academy surrounded by landscapes warped by sustained magical activity — stone twisted into unnatural formations, weather patterns locked in localized loops, flora and fauna mutated by ambient sorcerous energy. The ruins of fallen schools dot the broader terrain, some partially reclaimed by nature, others still radiating enough residual power to warp their surroundings centuries after their schools' destruction.

## §government

Each school is autonomous, governed by its master sorcerers in hierarchies determined by patronal favor and magical capability. There is no inter-school authority, no council, no treaty organization. Alliances and feuds shift constantly based on patronal politics, resource competition, and personal rivalries among sorcerers. Power is measured in two currencies: the depth of one's connection to a patron and the breadth of one's practical magical capability.

### Key Schools for NYC

**School of Tsathmat**
Patron: The Hammersea meteorite — or the entity communicating through it. Specializes in divination and temporal perception.

*Contact NPC — Shael:* Young diviner-priest, early 30s. Genuinely friendly by Pact standards, which makes him deeply unsettling by NYC standards. Functionally insane — hears the meteorite's voice constantly, carries on half-conversations with it mid-sentence. A valuable ally and a profoundly unreliable narrator. Cannot always distinguish between what he has divined, what the meteorite has told him, and what he has imagined.

Wants from NYC: ward-bypass services into fallen school ruins, theological validation (believes NYC's transport to this world was divinely ordained by the Hammersea), intelligence on gallery-level magical phenomena.
Offers: enchanted wards (anti-Verdancy growth suppression fields), healing services, basic magical instruction for gifted NYC citizens, divination consultations. Most generous payment terms of any Pact contact.

**School of Xophet**
Patron: Hasad-Krii, the Kestrel Demon. Specializes in storm, water, and wind sorcery.

*Contact NPC — Kadric:* Cold, professional sorcerer. Engages in business transactions only — no small talk, no relationship building, no favors. Paradoxically both more trustworthy (transparent, consistent motives) and less trustworthy (zero moral constraints on methods) than Shael. Every word he speaks is chosen with contract-precise deliberation; assume anything he says could be legally binding in Pact tradition.

Wants from NYC: exclusive ward-bypass contracts for specific fallen school ruins, intelligence on Outsider military artifacts.
Offers: ward-bypass contract work, storm-sorcery services including targeted lightning strikes against Verdancy incursions.

## §culture

Pact culture is scholastic, competitive, and ruthlessly hierarchical. Apprentices serve masters for decades. Knowledge is currency — hoarded, traded, stolen. Each school's culture is inflected by its patron: Tsathmat's diviners are mystical and prophetic, Xophet's storm-sorcerers are precise and contractual. Social interaction between schools follows elaborate protocols designed to prevent accidental magical offense. Betrayal is expected and planned for; trust is a tactical resource, not a moral value. The Pact has no unified cultural identity — only a shared tradition of patronal binding and a mutual recognition that the schools' survival depends on maintaining certain baseline conventions.

## §sensory-palette

Warped landscapes surround school-cities — stone bent into spirals, sky tinged with unnatural hues, ozone-charged air that tastes of metal and static. Geometric sigils are carved into every surface: walls, floors, door frames, furniture. The ambient feel is of power barely contained, pressure against the skin like standing near a high-voltage transformer. Fallen school ruins carry a different atmosphere: overgrown fortress walls, the shimmer of dormant wards at thresholds like heat-haze in cold air, deep silence broken only by wind through empty corridors.

## §voice-notes

Pact sorcerers speak with theatrical authority — every statement delivered as though pronouncing a verdict. Shael is the exception: he babbles enthusiastically about theological signs and divine patterns, interrupting himself to relay the meteorite's commentary, shifting between lucid insight and incomprehensible mysticism mid-sentence. Kadric speaks in contract-precise language where every word carries specific legal weight in Pact tradition. When dealing with outsiders, Pact sorcerers default to a formal register that sounds archaic — "we would propose," "the terms are thus," "let the binding witness."

## §adventure-hooks

- Shael sends urgent word that his divinations show a fallen school ruin is about to be breached by a rival surviving school. If NYC sends a ward-bypass team first, they can claim the most valuable artifacts before the rivals arrive — but the timeline is days, not weeks.
- Kadric offers an unusually generous contract: storm-sorcery support against the next Verdancy siege in exchange for a single item retrieved from a specific fallen school ruin. He will not explain why he wants it or what it does.
- A previously unknown Pact school has re-emerged after centuries of presumed destruction. Its sorcerers are making contact with NYC directly, bypassing Shael and Kadric. Both existing contacts react with alarm and conflicting advice.
- An NYC scavenging team returns from a fallen school ruin carrying an artifact that begins broadcasting a patronal signal — effectively advertising NYC's location to every Pact school that can detect it. Removing or silencing the artifact requires expertise NYC does not have.
