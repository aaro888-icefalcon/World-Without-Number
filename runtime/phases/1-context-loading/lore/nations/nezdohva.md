# Nezdohva

## §history
Nezdohva is a vast, cold empire that sprawls across the northern forests and tundra. It was united two centuries ago by the Iron Tsarina, a warrior-queen who conquered the feuding principalities through sheer military force and an iron will. Her descendants rule still, maintaining control through a network of secret police, military garrisons, and strategic marriages. Nezdohva is feared by its neighbors — it is the largest nation in the Latter Earth by territory, though much of that territory is uninhabitable wilderness.

## §geography
Enormous boreal forests, frozen tundra, and icy rivers stretching from the Pale Heights to the Nightwater coast. The land is flat in the west, rising to low hills in the east. Winters last eight months; summers are brief but intense, with nearly endless daylight. The forests are vast and trackless, home to wolves, bears, and worse. Scattered Legacy ruins lie half-buried in permafrost.

## §government
An autocratic monarchy. The Tsar rules with absolute authority, supported by a hereditary aristocracy and a feared secret service — the Ochrana. Provincial governors answer directly to the throne. The military is enormous but poorly equipped outside elite units. Serfdom was abolished a generation ago, but its legacy persists in practice.

## §culture
Nezdohvan culture is fatalistic, deeply emotional beneath a stoic exterior, and shaped by the harshness of the land. Communal suffering is a bonding experience. Literature and music are powerful and melancholic. Vodka distilled from birch sap is the universal drink. Hospitality is generous once trust is established, but outsiders are viewed with suspicion. The Orthodox Church of the Eternal Flame is a major cultural force.

## §cultural-implications
Endurance and toughness earn respect. Nezdohvan NPCs are initially suspicious but become fiercely loyal once befriended. Displays of vulnerability can paradoxically build trust — the Nezdohvans respect honesty about hardship. Authority figures expect deference. The Ochrana is everywhere; careless words have consequences.

## §sensory-palette
Nezdohva is white and grey — snow-covered forests under leaden skies, birch bark peeling in the cold wind. The air bites with cold that seeps through any clothing. Indoors, everything is warmth: crackling fires, amber lamplight, heavy furs. The smell of pine resin, black bread, and wood smoke defines home. In the far north, the sky burns with strange lights — auroras that some say carry voices.

## §voice-notes
Nezdohvans speak with heavy emphasis on consonants and a rolling, rhythmic cadence. They use proverbs constantly: "The wolf that runs alone dies in the snow" and "God is high and the Tsar is far." They are sparse with words in public but eloquent in private. Toasts before drinking are elaborate and sincere.

## §adventure-hooks
- A military outpost on the northern frontier has gone silent. The Tsar's agents suspect desertion; the local governor suspects something came out of the frozen ruins nearby.
- The Ochrana has discovered a conspiracy within the aristocracy, but the conspirators appear to be acting under magical compulsion. The source of the compulsion is unknown.
- A thaw has exposed a perfectly preserved Third Age settlement in the permafrost. The buildings are intact. So are some of the inhabitants — frozen but showing signs of potential revival.
- Religious pilgrims in the north report that the Eternal Flame — a sacred natural gas vent that has burned for centuries — is changing color and speaking in tongues.

## §unknown
[UNKNOWN] — The northern auroras are not a natural phenomenon. They intensify near certain Legacy installations and produce effects on living minds — visions, compulsions, euphoria. The Tsar's government has been quietly studying them for decades. What they have learned is classified at the highest level.
