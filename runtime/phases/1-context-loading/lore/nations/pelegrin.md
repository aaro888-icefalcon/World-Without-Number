# Pelegrin

## §history

Pelegrin spent centuries as a middling Amundi kingdom until King Gautier ascended to the throne two decades ago. Through patient, methodical expansion — diplomatic marriages, economic absorption, and selective military campaigns — Gautier has transformed Pelegrin into the dominant military power among the Amundi kingdoms. Each annexation followed the same pattern: assessment, economic pressure, diplomatic isolation, confrontation. Gautier does not bluff, and he has never lost. His ambition is Pelegrinian hegemony over all of Amund, and NYC's sudden appearance represents both an obstacle and an opportunity in that long-term calculus.

## §geography

Pelegrin controls extensive territory east of Mishar, encompassing fertile lowlands worked by serf labor, stone fortress towns, and strategic mountain passes. The capital houses Gautier's court and the kingdom's military command structure. Population is approximately 2 million, organized into a rigid feudal hierarchy. The landscape is productive but austere — ordered fields, military roads, and fortifications dominate the terrain. Pelegrin's borders press against Mishar to the west and other Amundi territories to the north and south.

## §government

King Gautier rules with absolute authority exercised through a disciplined bureaucracy and military command structure. He speaks softly, plans years ahead, respects competence, and despises waste. The nobility serves at his pleasure and understands that failure is career-ending at best. The serf-labor economy produces surplus that funds the strongest Amundi military: 15,000 to 20,000 standing professional soldiers supplemented by 40,000 or more levy troops. Gautier does not hate NYC — he simply cannot allow a rival power to consolidate on his strategic flank.

## §culture

Pelegrinian culture values order, discipline, efficiency, and service to the crown. The serf class works the land under strict obligations; the nobility administers and commands; the military trains constantly. Art and learning are tolerated insofar as they serve state interests. Court life revolves around whispered calculation — who is rising, who is falling, what the king intends. Personal displays of wealth or ambition are considered vulgar; quiet competence is the highest social currency.

## §sensory-palette

- Ordered fields worked by serfs in coordinated rows, the landscape a patchwork of geometric precision
- Stone fortresses on hilltops and at crossroads, banners snapping in the wind
- Disciplined soldiers marching in formation, boots striking cobblestone in unison
- Gautier's court: low voices, careful gestures, the scratch of quills recording every word
- Military encampments along the border — cook fires, weapon maintenance, the quiet tension of readiness
- Supply wagons moving in regular convoys along well-maintained roads

## §voice-notes

Gautier speaks softly, precisely, and without wasted words. His questions are more dangerous than his commands. Court speech mirrors the king: understated, deliberate, layered with implication. Military officers are crisply formal. Common soldiers speak in clipped, efficient phrases. Pelegrinians abroad maintain this restraint, making them difficult to read. When a Pelegrinian raises their voice, the situation has already been decided.
