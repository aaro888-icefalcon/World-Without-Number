# Morvassi

## §history
The Morvassi are a seafaring people without a fixed homeland. Their nation is their fleet — hundreds of ships ranging from small fishing boats to massive multi-hulled vessels that serve as floating towns. Driven from their original homeland by a catastrophe three centuries ago (the details are preserved in oral tradition but not shared with outsiders), the Morvassi took to the sea and never returned to land permanently. They trade, fish, and occasionally raid, moving with the seasons and the currents. Every port knows them; none claims them.

## §geography
The open sea — primarily the Sunward Sea and the Jade Gulf, though Morvassi fleets have been sighted in every navigable body of water. Their "territory" is wherever their fleet currently sails. Temporary camps on uninhabited coastlines serve for repairs and resupply. The fleet gathers annually at a secret location for the Great Muster, where marriages are arranged, disputes settled, and the coming year's routes negotiated.

## §government
A fleet democracy. Each ship is governed by its captain, elected by the crew. Captains' councils make decisions for fleet sections. The Admiral of the Muster — elected annually — coordinates the Great Muster and represents the Morvassi in dealings with land nations. No permanent hierarchy exists beyond the individual ship.

## §culture
Morvassi culture is maritime, egalitarian, and fiercely independent. The sea is home, temple, and livelihood. Every Morvassi learns to sail, swim, and fish before they can read. Oral storytelling preserves history, law, and navigational knowledge. Music is central — every task has an accompanying song. Tattoos record personal history and achievements. Trust is earned through shared voyages. Morvassi are generous to friends and merciless to enemies.

## §cultural-implications
Sailing, swimming, and survival skill checks are valued. Morvassi NPCs judge outsiders by their seaworthiness and honesty. Offering to share work aboard a vessel earns respect. Displays of wealth impress less than demonstrations of skill. Morvassi trade fairly but negotiate ruthlessly. Their information about sea routes and coastal features is priceless.

## §sensory-palette
The Morvassi world is deck planks and open sky — the creak of rigging, the snap of sail canvas, the endless rhythm of waves. The air is pure salt and wind. Ships are painted in bright colors, decks strung with lanterns and drying nets. Cooking smoke rises from galley stoves. Music is ever-present: work songs, love ballads, the percussive stamp of dancing feet on wooden decks.

## §voice-notes
Morvassi speak with a rolling, rhythmic cadence shaped by life on the waves. They use nautical language exclusively: "weather the storm" for enduring hardship, "anchor" for trust. Common greeting: "What sea brings you?" They are loud, expressive, and physically affectionate with friends. They sing more than they talk.

## §adventure-hooks
- The Great Muster has been disrupted by the appearance of a ship that no Morvassi built — enormous, made of metal, and apparently unmanned. It matches descriptions from the oral tradition of the fleet that destroyed their original homeland.
- A Morvassi captain has discovered an uncharted island that perfectly matches the description of their lost homeland. The Admiral has forbidden anyone from landing. The captain is considering defiance.
- Something is following the southern fleet — glimpsed beneath the waves, enormous and slow. It has been tracking them for weeks. The Morvassi do not flee, but they are preparing.
- A land nation has declared Morvassi ships pirates and begun seizing them. The Morvassi need allies, but their pride makes asking difficult.

## §unknown
[UNKNOWN] — The catastrophe that drove the Morvassi from their homeland was not natural. Their oral tradition — shared only among captains at the Great Muster — describes a day when the sea itself rose against their island nation, animated by a will that was not human. The Morvassi did not simply flee; they were exiled by something that still inhabits the deep waters of their former home. Whether it knows they still sail, and whether it cares, is the fear that keeps the Admiral awake at night.
