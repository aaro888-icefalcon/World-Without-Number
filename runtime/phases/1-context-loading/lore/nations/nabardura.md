# Nabardura

## §history

Nabardura was once the mightiest of the Amundi kingdoms, its Great Kings ruling from Capavan over a unified territory stretching across fertile river valleys and trade crossroads. Centuries of succession crises, satrap rebellions, and external pressure fragmented central authority until the Great King became a nominal figurehead. Today the kingdom persists in name, held together by tradition and the shared fiction of royal supremacy, while competing satraps carve out autonomous domains. Capavan itself has partially emptied, its grand palaces abandoned as power migrated to satrap capitals.

## §geography

The largest Amundi territory by population (approximately 2.5 million) and land area. Capavan, the nominal capital, sits amid once-magnificent infrastructure now falling into disrepair — aqueducts, ceremonial roads, and palace complexes. The satrap capitals are scattered across the territory, each controlling productive agricultural land and trade routes. The landscape varies from river lowlands to rolling hills, with enough arable land to support a significant diaspora community drawn from NYC's displaced population.

## §government

The Great King in Capavan holds nominal authority but commands neither army nor revenue sufficient to enforce edicts. Real power resides with competing satraps, each ruling their domain as a minor kingdom. Satrap interests diverge sharply: some seek labor and trade from NYC, others want to use NYC as a political tool against rival satraps or external threats. Alliances shift constantly. The court in Capavan maintains elaborate protocol that masks its irrelevance.

## §culture

Nabardura's fragmentation has produced a patchwork of local cultures united by shared language and nostalgia for past greatness. The abandoned palaces of Capavan attract treasure-seekers, scholars, and squatters in equal measure. Bazaars in satrap capitals bustle with trade goods from across the Amundi world. The largest NYC diaspora community has formed here, drawn by available land and the satraps' willingness to accept newcomers as political assets.

## §sensory-palette

- Crumbling palace facades in Capavan, stone carvings worn smooth by centuries of rain
- Overgrown courtyards where fig trees split marble floors and birds nest in throne rooms
- Bustling bazaars in satrap capitals — dyed fabrics, copper work, the shouts of competing merchants
- Dusty ceremonial roads still arrow-straight but cracked and weed-choked between satrap domains
- The Great King's diminished court: echoing halls, threadbare tapestries, aging attendants maintaining ritual

## §voice-notes

Formal court speech varies dramatically by satrap. Each speaks as a minor king, with distinctive rhetorical flourishes and regional idioms. The Great King's court maintains an archaic formal register that other Amundi find pretentious. Common people code-switch between local satrap dialect and a shared Nabarduran trade pidgin. Diaspora NYC residents have begun blending English with Nabarduran expressions.

## §adventure-hooks

- A satrap offers NYC a permanent settlement grant in exchange for exclusive military alliance against a rival satrap — accepting means making enemies of other Nabarduran factions
- Explorers report that Capavan's abandoned palaces contain pre-Amundi artifacts of significant power, but the palace depths are dangerous and politically contested
- The Great King secretly contacts NYC leadership, proposing to legitimize their sovereignty in exchange for military support to reassert royal authority over the satraps
- NYC diaspora members in Nabardura send word that a satrap is conscripting them into forced labor, demanding rescue or diplomatic intervention
