# Blackport

## §history
Blackport is a free city with no laws — or rather, with only one: the Compact, which states that no external power may claim sovereignty over the city. Everything else is negotiable. Originally a smuggler's cove hidden in a maze of sea caves, Blackport grew into a thriving den of piracy, black-market trade, and criminal enterprise. Today it is the Latter Earth's largest black market, a place where anything can be bought, sold, or arranged for the right price. Governments publicly condemn it and privately use it.

## §geography
Built into and around a network of sea caves at the base of coastal cliffs, extending onto a sheltered natural harbor. The city is vertical and labyrinthine — tunnels, bridges, and platforms connected by ladders, ropes, and precarious walkways. The harbor is hidden from open water by rock formations. The climate is mild and damp. The surrounding coastline is deliberately kept wild to discourage approach by land.

## §government
Anarchy moderated by self-interest. There is no government. The Compact is enforced by collective action — any attempt to impose rule triggers a unified response. Disputes are settled by negotiation, bribery, or violence. Power belongs to whoever can hold it. The closest thing to authority is the Harbormasters' Guild, which controls dock access and maintains a rough peace in the commercial areas.

## §culture
Blackport's culture is transactional. Trust is earned through repeated fair dealing, not given freely. Every interaction is a negotiation. Loyalty is real but personal — to individuals, not institutions. The arts favor shanties, tattoo work, and the oral tradition of "the long con" — stories of legendary swindles retold with admiration. Food is whatever the ships bring in. Diversity is total; every culture, species, and persuasion is represented.

## §cultural-implications
Streetwise, deception, and commerce skill checks are essential. Blackport NPCs respect cunning and competence. Moral appeals are ineffective; practical arguments work. Reputation is everything — cheating someone and getting caught is fatal to future business. The city has its own rough justice, and thieves who steal from other thieves are dealt with harshly.

## §sensory-palette
Blackport is dark, damp, and raucous. Torchlight flickers off wet cave walls. The air is a stew of salt, bilge water, cooking grease, and exotic contraband. Voices in a dozen languages echo through the tunnels. Music — rough, cheerful, and slightly threatening — pours from tavern doorways. The harbor creaks with the rigging of ships that fly no flag.

## §voice-notes
Blackporters speak in a polyglot slang that borrows from every language. They are cheerfully direct about dishonest things: "Let's discuss terms" means negotiation; "Let's discuss terms privately" means the topic is illegal. Common expressions: "Everything has a price" and "The Compact keeps." They never use real names with strangers.

## §adventure-hooks
- A foreign navy is blockading Blackport, demanding the surrender of a specific fugitive. The Compact has been invoked — no one will hand over anyone. But the fugitive is genuinely dangerous, and the blockade is strangling trade.
- A Legacy artifact capable of controlling weather has surfaced in the markets. Multiple buyers are bidding, including agents of nations that would use it as a weapon. The current holder is playing them against each other.
- Someone is counterfeiting Blackport reputation tokens — the informal credit system that makes the black market function. If trust collapses, so does the city.
- A tunnel in the deepest part of the cave system has broken through into a Legacy structure. The first explorers came back wealthy. The second group did not come back at all.

## §unknown
[UNKNOWN] — The sea caves that form Blackport's foundation are not natural. Their regularity, their structural stability, and their resistance to erosion suggest they were carved — but by what, and for what purpose, is unknown. The deepest caves contain markings that predate all known civilizations. Occasionally, at extreme low tide, sounds emerge from passages that lead down beneath the sea floor.
