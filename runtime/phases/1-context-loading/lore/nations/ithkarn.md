# Ithkarn

## §history
Ithkarn is a necrocracy — a nation ruled by the undead. Three centuries ago, the sorcerer Ithkar achieved a form of intelligent undeath and offered the same transformation to his inner circle. Together, they conquered the surrounding territories with armies that did not tire, fear, or die permanently. The living population submitted, and an uneasy accommodation has persisted ever since. The Deathless Lords rule; the living serve, but are not enslaved — Ithkar understood that productive subjects are more useful than terrorized ones.

## §geography
A cold, overcast land of dark forests, peat bogs, and grey stone. The coast faces the Nightwater, bringing frequent fog and freezing rain. The soil is poor but workable. Rivers run dark with tannins from the vast peatlands. Legacy ruins are common, and many have been repurposed by the Deathless Lords as strongholds.

## §government
A necrocratic oligarchy. The seven surviving Deathless Lords each govern a province with absolute authority. They convene rarely, as they have eternity to settle disputes. Beneath them, living administrators handle daily governance. The living population has customary rights that the Lords generally respect — not from compassion, but from pragmatism.

## §culture
Ithkarni culture is somber, fatalistic, and quietly resilient. The living have adapted to their strange rulers with dark humor and grim practicality. Death is not feared as elsewhere — it is simply a change in employment status. Art favors dirges, memorial sculpture, and intricate bone carving. Funerary customs are elaborate, as the distinction between death and service is thin.

## §cultural-implications
Undead-related knowledge checks are critically important. PCs must navigate the tension between the Deathless Lords and the living population. Necromancy is legal here but still feared. Social checks with living Ithkarni require acknowledging their reality without pitying them.

## §sensory-palette
Ithkarn is grey and cold. Mist hangs in the dark pine forests, and the sun rarely breaks through the overcast sky. The air smells of peat smoke, damp earth, and a faint sweetness that is best not investigated. Silence predominates — the Deathless Lords do not generate the ambient noise of the living.

## §voice-notes
Ithkarni speak quietly, with a dry, understated delivery. Gallows humor is constant: "At least the Lords don't eat much" and "Death is just a career change." They refer to the undead as "the Cold Ones" in casual speech. They are polite to strangers but volunteer nothing.

## §adventure-hooks
- One of the Deathless Lords has been destroyed — truly destroyed, not merely damaged. The other Lords suspect a weapon from a prior age and want it found before it is used again.
- A living resistance movement is smuggling people out of Ithkarn. The Lords tolerate small-scale emigration but this operation is too large to ignore.
- A massive peat bog has drained overnight, revealing a battlefield from a prior age. The dead are stirring, and they do not answer to the Deathless Lords.
- A living administrator has discovered evidence that Ithkar's original transformation was flawed and the Deathless Lords are slowly losing their minds.

## §unknown
[UNKNOWN] — The nature of Ithkar's undeath is unique. No other necromancer has replicated it despite centuries of effort. Ithkar's original notes are sealed in his personal vault. Whether he made a pact with something, discovered a Legacy process, or achieved something entirely novel is unknown even to the other Deathless Lords.
