# Cindervale

## §history
Cindervale occupies the aftermath of a catastrophe. Two centuries ago, a volcanic eruption of unnatural intensity — almost certainly triggered by a Legacy system — buried a prosperous kingdom under ash and lava. The survivors rebuilt in the devastated landscape, discovering that the volcanic soil was extraordinarily fertile and the geothermal vents provided limitless energy. Cindervale is now a nation defined by its relationship with destruction — they farm the ashes, build on the lava, and treat the volcano as both provider and threat.

## §geography
A wide valley dominated by Mount Cinder, an active volcano surrounded by lava fields of varying age. The oldest fields support lush vegetation; the newest are barren black rock. Hot springs, geysers, and fumaroles are common. The soil is rich and dark. The climate is warm due to geothermal activity, with frequent ashfall. The air quality varies — some days clear, others hazy with volcanic particulates.

## §government
A council of Ashwardens — elected leaders responsible for monitoring volcanic activity, managing evacuations, and coordinating agriculture. The Chief Ashwarden holds executive authority during eruptions. Between eruptions, governance is communal and decentralized. Volcanological knowledge is the primary qualification for leadership.

## §culture
Cindervalians are fatalistic, resourceful, and darkly humorous. They live with the constant awareness that their home could destroy them at any moment, and this produces a culture that values the present intensely. Festivals are frequent and passionate. Art is ephemeral by philosophy — sand painting, ice sculpture, temporary installations. Food is excellent, grown in the rich volcanic soil. The phrase "nothing lasts" is not pessimism but a statement of values.

## §cultural-implications
Survival and nature-related skill checks are valued. Cindervalian NPCs are pragmatic and unimpressed by wealth or power — the volcano does not care about either. Generosity is respected; hoarding is despised. PCs who appreciate the present moment fit in well. Those who plan obsessively for the future are viewed with pity.

## §sensory-palette
Cindervale smells of sulfur, hot earth, and the green tang of fast-growing vegetation. Steam rises from fissures in the ground. The light has a yellowish cast from airborne particulates. At night, the volcano's glow paints the clouds orange. The sound of bubbling mud pots and hissing vents provides a constant background. Everything is warm to the touch — even the stones.

## §voice-notes
Cindervalians speak with warmth and urgency, as though every conversation might be their last. They use volcanic metaphors: "That plan is still cooling" and "Don't build on fresh lava." Common greeting: "Good ash today" (meaning the ashfall is light). They laugh at danger and cry at beauty, often simultaneously.

## §adventure-hooks
- Mount Cinder's eruption pattern has changed. The Ashwardens' models no longer predict its behavior. Something is interfering with the Legacy systems that partially control the volcano.
- A lava flow has uncovered a Legacy installation that the eruption two centuries ago was meant to destroy. Someone triggered that eruption deliberately, and the installation is still functional.
- A community built on an older lava field is sinking. Investigation reveals hollow chambers beneath — not natural lava tubes, but constructed spaces containing preserved artifacts from the buried kingdom.
- The volcano has begun producing a new mineral — crystalline, warm, and faintly luminous. It has remarkable properties. And something is emerging from the deep vents to collect it.

## §unknown
[UNKNOWN] — Mount Cinder is not a natural volcano. Deep geological surveys reveal a Legacy structure at its core — an engine of some kind that generates the volcanic activity. The eruption that created Cindervale was not an accident but an activation event. Whether the engine is malfunctioning, performing as designed, or building toward a specific output is the question that haunts the Ashwardens' private councils.
