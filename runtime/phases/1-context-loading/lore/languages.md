# Languages of the Latter Earth

## §overview

Language in the Latter Earth reflects millennia of cultural collision, conquest, and isolation. Every region carries linguistic sediment from prior ages — loan words from dead empires, place names whose meanings are lost, and scholarly terms borrowed from tongues no living person speaks natively. Communication across the Latter Earth is possible but rarely effortless.

## §trade-tongues

- **Common Varenthi**: The most widely spoken trade language, descended from the merchant pidgin of the Varenthar river cities. Functional rather than elegant. Understood in most major ports and trade towns across the temperate heartlands. Adequate for commerce and basic communication but lacks vocabulary for technical, magical, or philosophical concepts.
- **Coastal Cant**: A simplified creole spoken along the Sunward Sea and Jade Gulf coastlines. Heavy nautical vocabulary. Mutually intelligible with Common Varenthi at a basic level but diverges sharply in idiom and pronunciation.
- **Dust Tongue**: The trade language of the Interior Wastes and southern drylands. Tonal, with a compact vocabulary designed for efficiency. Caravan traders learn it by necessity.

## §regional-languages

- **High Athkari**: The prestige language of the eastern courts. Complex grammar, extensive honorific system. Speaking it marks one as educated or aristocratic.
- **Braldan**: A harsh, consonant-heavy language of the northern nations. Several regional dialects exist, some barely mutually intelligible.
- **Montaignard**: A melodic western language with strong literary traditions. Considered the language of diplomacy in the western nations.
- **Thalassic**: Spoken by the island and coastal cultures. Rich in maritime terminology. Contains many words with no equivalent in inland languages.
- **Yelangzi**: A tonal language of the far eastern regions. Logographic script. Extremely difficult for non-native speakers to master.
- **Gharistani**: A guttural language of the steppe peoples. Strong oral tradition; written form is relatively recent.
- **Sareni**: A flowing southern language known for its poetry. Uses a syllabic script of great beauty.

## §dead-languages

- **Old Titanic**: Fragments survive in inscriptions on Second Age ruins. No living person reads it fluently. Scholars have reconstructed partial vocabularies, but pronunciation is entirely conjectural. Some inscriptions cause discomfort or disorientation when read aloud — [UNKNOWN] whether this is a property of the language itself or residual enchantment.
- **Sorcerer-King Glyphic**: The formal language of the Third Age rulers. Better preserved than Old Titanic but enormously complex. Full fluency requires years of study. Most magical texts of significance are written in this script.
- **Pre-Shattering Standard**: The administrative language of the late Fourth Age. Readable by trained scholars. Most surviving bureaucratic records and census data are in this tongue.
- **Ur-Tongue**: A hypothetical proto-language that some scholars believe all other languages descend from. Its existence is [UNKNOWN] — debated fiercely in academic circles.

## §magical-and-scholarly-languages

- **Lexicon Arcana**: Not a natural language but a constructed notation system used by mages to record spells, formulae, and arcane theory. Requires magical training to parse. Reading it without understanding is dangerous — the symbols interact with the reader's mind.
- **Celestial Notation**: A mathematical-symbolic language found in the oldest Legacy astronomical instruments. Partially decoded. Used by navigators and astrologers who have access to pre-Shattering star charts.
- **Church Literate**: The liturgical language shared by several major religious traditions, descended from Pre-Shattering Standard but heavily ritualized. Clergy across distant nations can communicate through it, though secular meaning is often lost in translation.

## §gameplay-effects

Language barriers have concrete mechanical and narrative consequences:

- **Communication**: PCs can communicate freely in languages they know. Attempting to communicate in an unknown language requires skill checks with increasing difficulty based on linguistic distance.
- **Lore Access**: Many clues, inscriptions, and documents are in languages the PC may not read. Deciphering them may require finding a translator, spending downtime studying, or using magical means.
- **Social Interactions**: Speaking a local language (rather than a trade tongue) grants advantage on social skill checks. Speaking only a trade tongue may mark the PC as an outsider. Speaking no common language makes most social interaction impossible without creative solutions.
- **Dead Languages**: Reading dead languages requires specific training or magical assistance. Misreading ancient texts can have consequences ranging from wasted time to activating dormant enchantments.
- **Literacy**: Literacy is not universal. Many common folk speak their regional tongue but cannot read. Written messages may need to be delivered verbally.
